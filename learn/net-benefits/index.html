Counterplans · 7 min read · intermediate

Net benefits (NB)

Plain version. A counterplan is the negative's own plan, offered instead of the affirmative's. The net benefit is the reason the counterplan is better. Most often it is a harm that the plan causes and the counterplan avoids. The net benefit gives the judge a reason to prefer the counterplan. It also shows that doing both plans together is worse than the counterplan alone. Without a net benefit, most counterplans are not a reason to vote negative.

The two jobs of a net benefit

Job 1: a reason to prefer the counterplan. If the counterplan solves the same problem as the plan, the judge needs a reason to pick one over the other. The net benefit is that reason.

Job 2: proof of competition. A counterplan must compete: the judge must have a real choice between it and the plan. The aff tests this with a permutation ("perm"): "Do both." The net benefit answers the perm. It shows that doing both still causes the harm, so the counterplan alone is better. See Competition and Permutations.

A net benefit has to do both jobs. A harm that the perm avoids does Job 1 against the plan, but not Job 2 against the perm.

The kinds of net benefit

KindWhat it saysExample (generic)
ExternalThe plan causes a harm. The counterplan does not."Federal spending forces cuts. State action does not."
InternalThe counterplan itself causes an extra good thing."The counterplan also trains state staff."
SolvencyThe counterplan solves the aff's problem better."States know local needs, so the program works better."

External net benefits

An external net benefit is a disadvantage (DA) that sits outside the counterplan. A DA has four parts: uniqueness, link, internal link and impact. See Disadvantages. The plan links to the DA, and the counterplan does not.

This is the most common kind. It is also the easiest to use for competition. The neg argues that the perm includes the plan, so the perm links to the DA too.

Internal net benefits

An internal net benefit comes from the counterplan itself. The counterplan does something good that the plan does not.

These have a known weak point. The perm includes the counterplan. So the perm usually gets the same extra good thing. If so, the internal net benefit does not prove the counterplan competes. Many judges expect the neg to explain why the perm cannot get the benefit. For example, the benefit may come only when the counterplan's actor acts alone.

Solvency net benefits

Some counterplans claim to solve the aff's problem better than the plan. This can be a reason to prefer the counterplan. But the same weak point applies: the perm may solve just as well, because it includes the counterplan. The neg needs to explain why doing both is worse.

How a net benefit is weighed

The net benefit is weighed against the solvency deficit: the part of the aff's case the counterplan cannot solve. See Solvency deficits.

  • If the counterplan solves the whole case, there is no deficit. Then even a small risk of the net benefit can decide the round, because the aff has nothing left to weigh against it.
  • If the counterplan misses a large part of the case, the net benefit must be bigger than that missing part.

Judges differ on small risks. Some judges think almost any risk of a net benefit beats a counterplan that solves everything. Others think a very small risk is close to zero, and they will not vote on it without a clear story. When both the deficit and the net benefit are tiny, some judges also ask which side has presumption: who wins when nothing else decides. See Presumption and Impact calculus.

How to answer a net benefit (for the aff)

  • Answer it as a DA. Non-unique, no link, no internal link, no impact. If the DA is false, the counterplan has no edge. Use the answers from Disadvantages.
  • Perm avoids it. Explain why doing both does not cause the harm.
  • The counterplan links too. Show that the counterplan also causes the harm. Then the net benefit is not a difference between them.
  • The perm gets it. Against an internal or solvency net benefit, say the perm includes the counterplan and so gets the same good thing.
  • Deficit outweighs. Even if the net benefit is true, the part of the case the counterplan misses is bigger.

How to extend a net benefit (for the neg)

  • Extend every part of the DA. A net benefit is a full argument. If a part is dropped or lost, the chain breaks.
  • Explain the link to the perm. Say clearly why doing both still causes the harm.
  • Weigh it. Compare the net benefit with the deficit. Say why the net benefit is bigger or more likely.

How to judge a net benefit

  1. Is it true? Judge it like any DA. What is the risk?
  2. Does the perm avoid it? If yes, the counterplan does not compete. Set the counterplan aside, but the DA can still count against the plan.
  3. Does the counterplan link too? If yes, the net benefit is not a reason to prefer the counterplan.
  4. Weigh. Compare the risk of the net benefit with the size of the deficit.

How to read it: the 1NC gave two net benefits. The second one was internal: the counterplan trains state staff. The 2AC said the perm gets that benefit too, because the perm includes the counterplan. The 2NR did not extend the second net benefit, so it is out of the round. The 2NR went for the spending DA and said the perm still has the federal cost. The 2AR did not deny that the perm has the cost. It argued the cost is small, so the DA risk is tiny. This flow shows no solvency deficit. So the judge asks how big the DA risk is, and whether any risk is enough when the counterplan solves the whole case.

Common mistakes

  • Neg: a net benefit the perm also gets. Fix: lead with an external net benefit, or explain why only the counterplan alone gets the benefit.
  • Neg: extending the counterplan but not the full DA. Fix: extend uniqueness, link, internal link and impact in the block and the 2NR.
  • Neg: a counterplan that links to its own net benefit. Fix: check that your counterplan text avoids the DA's link.
  • Aff: answering the counterplan but ignoring the net benefit. Fix: answer the DA too. If it falls, the counterplan has no edge.
  • Aff: claiming a deficit without weighing it. Fix: compare the deficit with the risk of the net benefit, out loud.
  • Judges: voting on a net benefit the neg never tied to the perm. Fix: check whether the perm avoids it, as argued.
