Answering a kritik (K)

8 min read · Intermediate

A kritik, or K, is a negative argument that questions the ideas behind the affirmative, and answering one well takes a plan.

  • First, in cross-ex: Learn what the K says and what its alternative does.
  • Then, in the 2AC: Answer each part: framework, the perm, the link, the impact and the alternative. Always explain why your case still matters.
  • Later speeches: Pick the best few answers and go deep on them.

Before the 2AC: listen and ask

The 1NC reads the kritik. Then the aff gets a cross-ex. Use it to pin down the K before you answer it. Good questions:

  • What does the alternative do? Who does it, and what changes?
  • Is the alt imagined to happen, or does it happen in this round? This tells you how to answer it. See Fiat.
  • Does the alt let us do the plan? If yes, the alt may be a PIK, a version that keeps the plan. You want to know now, not in the last speech.
  • Which part of our 1AC is the link? Make them name it.
  • Is the K conditional? That is, can the neg drop it later?
  • What is the role of the ballot? What must each team do to win?

Write the answers on your flow. If the neg changes its story later, you can point back to cross-ex.

The 2AC: a common order

There is no single right order. Many teams write a prepared set of answers before the tournament. Some call it a 2AC block. (It is not the same as the "neg block," the 2NC and 1NR back to back.) This order is common:

StepAnswerWhat it does
1FrameworkSays the judge should weigh the plan's results
2PermSays the aff can do the plan and the alt together
3Link answersSays the aff does not do what the K says, or does the opposite
4Impact answersSays the K's harm is smaller or less likely than claimed
5Alt answersSays the alt does not solve, or is too vague to judge
6Case outweighsSays the aff's advantages are bigger than the K
7Theory, if it fitsFor example, conditionality, or an objection to a vague or floating alt

How long the K gets depends on what else the 1NC read. Make sure each step has at least one real argument.

Step 1: Framework

Framework is the debate about what the judge weighs. The aff usually argues that the judge should weigh the plan's results against the present system or a competing policy. This is a big topic with its own page. See Framework against a kritik.

Framework alone is rarely enough. If you lose it and have nothing else, you lose the round.

Step 2: The perm

A permutation (perm) tests whether the alt is a reason to reject the plan. Common perms:

  • Perm do both: do the plan and the alt together.
  • Perm do the plan and the parts of the alt that do not conflict with it.

Say what the perm looks like in one sentence. The neg will usually answer that the K's links are reasons the perm fails. Be ready to explain why doing both is better than the alt alone. See Permutations.

Step 3: Link answers

Answer each link the neg read. Use no link where the aff truly does not do or say the thing. Use link turns where the aff works against the harmful idea. Point out generic links that would apply to any aff. See The kritik link.

Step 4: Impact answers

You can argue the K's impact is not as big or as likely as claimed. You can also argue the case outweighs it.

A few affs impact turn: they argue that the idea the K criticizes is actually good. This can work, but it is risky. Be careful not to combine it with a perm. If you say the idea is good, you cannot also say you will do an alt that rejects that idea.

Step 5: Alt answers

  • Alt fails: the alt does not solve the K's impact.
  • Alt cannot solve the case: even if it works, it does nothing about the aff's harms.
  • Alt is too vague: the judge cannot tell what it does. Some affs add a theory argument here.
  • Floating PIK: if the alt might include the plan, say that this should have been clear in the 1NC.

See The alternative.

Step 6: Case outweighs

This is the step new teams forget most. Explain why the aff's advantages are bigger, more likely or sooner than the K's impact. Also answer "K turns case": the neg's claim that the aff's way of thinking makes its own advantages fail. If that claim stands, your case may be worth nothing. See Turns case.

Avoid contradictions

Some answers fight each other. Check your 2AC for these:

  • An impact turn (the idea is good) plus a perm with an alt that rejects the idea.
  • A link turn (the aff fights the idea) plus an impact turn (the idea is good). Together, they say the aff fights a good thing.

The neg will point these out. Pick the answers that tell one story.

The 1AR: keep what matters

The neg block often spends a lot of time on the K. The 1AR is short, so it must choose. Most 1ARs keep:

  • framework,
  • the perm, with an answer to "links are reasons the perm fails,"
  • the best one or two link or alt answers,
  • case weighing, with an answer to "K turns case."

Do not drop the neg's best arguments. A dropped "K turns case" or a dropped perm answer often decides the round.

The 2AR: pick a story

The 2AR cannot win everything. Pick one main path, and add "even if" layers.

  • Framework and case: "Weigh the plan. Under that test, the case clearly outweighs."
  • Perm and case: "Even if ideas matter, the perm gets the alt's benefits and the plan's."
  • Link and case: "We do not link, so the K is not a reason to reject this aff."

Then layer: "Even if you weigh ideas first, the perm solves. Even if the perm fails, the case outweighs."

For more on final speeches, see Rebuttal strategy.

For judges

When the aff answers a K, check three things. Did the aff answer the link and the perm, not only framework? Did the aff compare its case to the K? And did the aff answer "K turns case"? The full steps for judging a K are on Kritiks.

For common aff answers to each kritik family, see Common kritik families.

Example in flow shorthand

A generic capitalism kritik against an aff that funds grants to reduce poverty.

Key: FW = framework, squo = status quo (the present system), mkt = market, adv = advantage, CX = cross-ex, Ext = extend (carry forward), "DAs to perm" = reasons the perm is worse. Each speech answers the 2AC's points in the same order.

     grant

  4. Alt = refuse mkt logic in
     every choice

     reaches few

1AR
  1. Ext FW: neg must have a
     policy option
  2. Perm: plan is one small
     step; alt does the rest
  3. Grants go to public
     bodies too
  4. Vague: no actor, no
     timeline
  5. (no answer)

2NR
  1. Ideas first; plan is
     built on mkt logic
  2. Perm still uses mkt;
     links outweigh
  3. Ext CX link
  4. Alt solves root cause of
     case harms
  5. Ext: K turns case,
     dropped by 1AR

2AR
  1. Even if ideas count, case
     outweighs
  2. Perm: plan + alt is best
     of both
  3. (not extended)
  4. Alt is still vague; no
     one acts
  5. Case still helps people
     now

How to read it: the 2AR goes for framework, the perm and case weighing, with an "even if" on point 1. It does not extend the no link argument on point 3, so the neg's cross-ex link stands. The key problem is point 5. The block said the K turns the case, and the 1AR did not answer. The 2NR points out the drop. The 2AR repeats that the case helps people now, but it never answers the turn directly, and a new answer in the 2AR would be too late for most judges. So the neg is ahead on whether the case still counts. The round likely turns on points 2 and 5: does the perm work, and how much of the case survives the turn?

Common mistakes

  • Aff: only framework. Fix: also make a perm, answer the links, and weigh the case.
  • Aff: a perm with no explanation. Fix: say what doing both looks like, and why it beats the alt alone.
  • Aff: answers that contradict. Fix: do not impact turn the idea and also perm an alt that rejects it.
  • Aff: forgetting the case. Fix: in every speech, say why the advantages outweigh the K.
  • Aff: dropping "K turns case" in the 1AR. Fix: answer it every time. If it stands, your case may count for nothing.
  • Aff: wasting cross-ex. Fix: ask what the alt does, whether it includes the plan, and where the link is.

4 short questions. Get them all right to finish this step. You can try again as many times as you want. No score is kept.

Find the perm

The aff's plan funds community health clinics. The neg reads a kritik that says the aff trusts distant experts over local people. The alt is to support local groups that decide their own health needs. The 2AC makes four answers.

Which answer is a permutation?

  • "Do the plan, and also support the local groups the alt names."
  • "Our clinics are run by local boards, so we work against expert control."
  • "The alt has no actor and no timeline."
  • "Weigh the plan's results against the present system."
Show the answer

Answer: "Do the plan, and also support the local groups the alt names."

Why: A permutation, or perm, says the aff can do the plan and the alt together. It tests whether the alt is a reason to reject the plan. The second answer is a link turn, the third is an alt answer, and the fourth is framework.

Find the link turn

The aff's plan gives housing money straight to tenants. The neg reads a kritik that says the aff relies on landlords and the market to fix housing. The 2AC makes four answers.

Which answer is a link turn?

  • "Do the plan, and also the alt in other areas."
  • "The plan moves money away from landlords and to tenants, so it works against the market control the kritik criticizes."
  • "The case saves people from losing their homes now, so it outweighs."
  • "This link would apply to any aff."
Show the answer

Answer: "The plan moves money away from landlords and to tenants, so it works against the market control the kritik criticizes."

Why: A link turn says the aff actually works against the thing the kritik criticizes. "No link" would say only that the aff does not do it. The first answer is a perm, the third is case weighing, and the fourth points out a generic link.

Answers that clash

The neg reads a kritik that says the aff treats economic growth as the goal. The 2AC lists several answers.

Which two answers contradict each other?

  • "Our plan slows growth, so we work against the idea the kritik criticizes" and "Growth is good"
  • "The alt has no actor" and "The case outweighs"
  • "Weigh the plan" and "The case outweighs"
  • "No link" and "The alt has no actor"
Show the answer

Answer: "Our plan slows growth, so we work against the idea the kritik criticizes" and "Growth is good"

Why: The first part is a link turn: the aff says it fights the idea. "Growth is good" is an impact turn: it says the idea is actually good. Together they say the aff fights a good thing. The neg will point this out. The other pairs can sit together in one 2AC.

A dropped turn

You flow this on the case and K sheets.

Block
  K turns case: aid through
    the market reaches few
1AR
  (no answer)
2NR
  Ext: K turns case, dropped

In words: in the block, the neg says the kritik turns the case: aid that goes through the market reaches few people. The 1AR does not answer. The 2NR points out the drop.

What is the main problem for the aff?

  • The aff can answer the turn for the first time in the 2AR
  • The neg's claim that the case fails stands, so the case may count for little
  • The perm is now gone
  • Nothing, because a turn only matters if it is read in the 1NC
Show the answer

Answer: The neg's claim that the case fails stands, so the case may count for little

Why: "K turns case" says the aff's way of thinking makes its own advantages fail. If the aff drops it, the case may count for little. A new answer in the 2AR would be too late for most judges. That is why the 1AR should answer it every time.
