The Tabula Debate Library

This is a free library about high school policy debate. It starts simple and goes deeper, one topic per page. Every page begins with a short plain summary, so you can stop there or keep going. Pick the path below that fits you. If you hit a word you do not know, check the glossary.

Pick your path

I am judging soon, and I am new (about 30 minutes)

You do not need debate experience to judge well. You need to listen, take notes, and decide from what the teams said.

  1. Debate 101: what happens in a round.
  2. Judging your first round: what to do before, during and after.
  3. How to flow: take notes you can decide from.
  4. Speaker points: how to score each debater.
  5. The ballot and the RFD: how to write your RFD (reason for decision).
  6. Impact calculus: how to compare arguments. Read the plain summary.

I am a new debater (your first month)

  1. Debate 101: what happens in a round.
  2. Speeches, order and times: who speaks when, and for how long.
  3. How to flow, then Flow shorthand: how to take notes.
  4. The affirmative case, then Stock issues: the aff's basic jobs.
  5. Disadvantages, then Answering a DA: a DA says the plan causes harm.
  6. Counterplans, then Permutations: the neg's other plan, and the aff's answer.
  7. Topicality: does the plan fit the topic?
  8. Cross-examination: how to ask and answer questions.
  9. Impact calculus: how to compare arguments.
  10. Rebuttal strategy: what to say in the last speeches.

About this library

  • Free, always. No sign-up, no email wall.
  • Fair to every style. Policy, kritikal, traditional and lay debate all get a fair, plain explanation. Where coaches disagree, the page says so.
  • Timeless. Examples are generic. They do not depend on this year's topic.
  • Local rules differ. Times, prep and point scales here are the common national format. Check your tournament's rules.
  • Share it. The text is licensed under CC BY-SA 4.0. You may copy it into a handout for your team, with credit.
  • Found a mistake? Open an issue on the Tabula GitHub page. Each page gets a review by an experienced coach before it goes live, and we fix errors fast.
