The round · 5 min read · beginner

Speaker points

Plain version. Speaker points are a score the judge gives each debater, apart from who wins. Most leagues use a scale near 25 to 30, with tenths allowed, such as 28.4. Higher is better. Points reward clear speaking, smart choices, good cross-ex and respect for others. Points help break ties in the standings and decide speaker awards, so being fair and consistent matters.

How the scale works

Every league sets its own scale. Always check the ballot or the tournament's instructions first. Some use whole numbers. Some use tenths or halves. Some use a different range. The guide below is for the common 30-point scale with tenths.

Rough guide (it varies by league and by level):

PointsWhat it usually means
29.5 – 30Outstanding. Among the best speeches you expect to see at this level. Rare.
29.0 – 29.4Excellent. Clear, smart and in control of the round.
28.5 – 28.9Very good. Strong choices, few errors.
28.0 – 28.4Good. Solid and clear, with some missed chances.
27.5 – 27.9Average for the level. Did the job, with real gaps.
27.0 – 27.4Below average. Hard to follow, or dropped key arguments.
26.0 – 26.9Weak round. Major problems with clarity or strategy.
Below 26Usually kept for serious problems, such as rude or offensive conduct. Many leagues ask the judge to explain.

Judge the level. A strong novice and a strong varsity debater can both earn a 29 in their own division. Compare each debater to others at the same level.

Point ranges drift. In many circuits, the average has moved up over time. Ask the tournament or a coach what a typical point is if you are not sure.

What earns points

  • Clarity. Can the judge understand the words and follow the order? Speed is fine only if it stays clear.
  • Organization. Roadmaps, signposts, and answers that match the flow.
  • Strategy. Good choices: answering every argument, going for the right things in rebuttals, not wasting time.
  • Explanation. Saying why, in the debater's own words, not just reading cards.
  • Cross-ex. Sharp questions and honest, clear answers.
  • Respect. Courtesy to opponents, partner and judge.

Points and the win

  • Points are separate from the decision. A debater on the losing team can earn the highest points in the round.
  • A "low-point win" is when the winning team gets lower total points than the losing team. Many tournaments forbid or flag a low-point win. Follow your tournament's rule.
  • Points should reflect this round, not a debater's reputation or record.

Giving points fairly

  1. Decide the winner first. Then give points.
  2. Use the same scale all day. Write down the points you gave in each round, so you stay consistent.
  3. Do not punish style alone. A debater who speaks in a way you do not prefer can still earn high points if they are clear and smart.
  4. Explain on the ballot. One or two lines per debater: one thing done well, one thing to work on.
  5. Never use points to punish who a debater is. Points rate the debating, not the person.

Example in flow shorthand

A judge's margin notes for points, written during the round.

Speaker      Notes (margin of flow)                           Pts
1A (1AC/1AR) clear, ext case well in 1AR; dropped IL on DA    28.3
2A (2AC/2AR) answered all off; strong 2AR weighing; 2AC slow  28.8
1N (1NC/1NR) fast, unclear tags; good T in 1NR                27.9
2N (2NC/2NR) great CX; 2NR well explained; 2NC ran long on T  28.7

How to read it: the judge notes one strength and one weakness for each debater, then gives points. The 2A and 2N are close. The 1N lost points for unclear tags, even though their topicality work was good.

Common mistakes

  • Giving the same points to everyone. Fix: use the range to show real differences. Points are useful only if they differ.
  • Matching points to the win every time. Fix: rate each speaker. The best speaker can be on the losing team.
  • Using a scale from a different league. Fix: read the ballot's instructions and use the local scale.
  • Low points with no reason. Fix: if you give a low score, explain why on the ballot.
  • Rating speed or style instead of clarity. Fix: ask whether you could follow the arguments, not whether you liked the style.

Clear or fast?

Speaker A reads many cards very fast. You cannot understand most of the tags, and the speech jumps between arguments. Speaker B reads fewer cards, speaks clearly, follows a roadmap, and explains why each argument matters.

Which speaker usually deserves higher points?

  • Speaker A, because they read more evidence
  • Speaker B, because the speech was clear, organized and explained
  • Both the same, because both spoke for the full time
  • Speaker A, because speed always shows skill
Show the answer

Answer: Speaker B, because the speech was clear, organized and explained

Why: Points reward clarity, organization and explanation. Speed is fine only if the judge can still follow it. If you could not understand the tags, those arguments did not help you decide.

Points and the win

Team X wins the round. But a debater on Team Y, the losing team, gave the best speeches in the round.

Can the debater on the losing team get the highest points?

  • Yes, because points rate each speaker apart from the decision
  • No, because the winning team must always get higher points
  • Only if the other judge agrees
  • Only if the tournament has no ballot
Show the answer

Answer: Yes, because points rate each speaker apart from the decision

Why: Points are separate from the win. A debater on the losing team can earn the highest points. Some tournaments forbid or flag a "low-point win", where the whole winning team has fewer points. Follow your tournament's rule.

Style you do not like

Speaker C talks in a calm, quiet style that you do not prefer. But the speech is clear, answers every argument, and makes smart choices in the rebuttal. Speaker D has a style you like, but drops two key arguments.

Which speaker usually deserves higher points?

  • Speaker C, because the debating was clear and smart
  • Speaker D, because you liked the style
  • Both the same, because style and strategy cancel out
  • Neither, because style should not get points
Show the answer

Answer: Speaker C, because the debating was clear and smart

Why: Do not punish style alone. Points rate clarity, strategy and explanation. Dropping key arguments is a real weakness. A style you do not prefer is not.

What points rate

You have heard that one debater has a strong record and is well known on the circuit.

What should your points for that debater rate?

  • Their record and how well known they are
  • How well they debated in this round
  • Who they are as a person
  • How their team did at the last tournament
Show the answer

Answer: How well they debated in this round

Why: Points rate this round only. They rate the debating, not the person, and not a reputation or a record.
