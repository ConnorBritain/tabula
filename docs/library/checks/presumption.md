## Nothing left on the flow

The neg defends the status quo (the world now, without the plan). It ran no counterplan.

```
2NR:
  No solvency: plan's program
    is too small (Analyst 21)
  No DA left
2AR:
  Program helps (Expert 22)
  (no answer to "too small")
```

In words: the neg says the plan's program is too small to solve, and the neg has no disadvantage left. The aff says the program helps, but never answers the point that it is too small.

You decide that the plan does not solve, so the aff has no advantage left. Neither team argued who gets presumption. Who do most judges vote for?

- [x] The neg, on presumption
- [ ] The aff, because it spoke last
- [ ] The aff, because the neg has no disadvantage
- [ ] Neither team, so the judge flips a coin

**Why:** Neither team has a reason to vote for it, so presumption decides. Most judges give presumption to the neg by default, because the aff asks for a change and must prove the change is good. The judge should say in the RFD that a default was used.

## A small risk

The neg wins that most of the plan's advantage is gone. But you decide there is still a small, real chance that the plan helps. The neg has no disadvantage. The 2NR asks you to vote neg on presumption.

What do most judges do?

- [ ] Vote neg on presumption, because the advantage is small
- [x] Vote aff, because a small risk of an advantage is still a reason to vote for the plan
- [ ] Vote neg, because the neg asked for presumption first
- [ ] Ignore both teams and use their own view of the plan

**Why:** Presumption decides only when no one has offense (a reason to vote for their side) left. A small risk of an advantage is offense. For most judges, it beats presumption. Some judges say in their paradigm that they rarely vote on presumption at all, for this reason.

## When the neg has a counterplan

The neg goes for a counterplan in the 2NR. The counterplan is a bigger change than the plan. Both teams end up with no offense left. The 2AR argues about presumption.

Which claim can the 2AR make about presumption?

- [x] "The counterplan is also a change, and a bigger one, so presumption should go to the aff."
- [ ] "Presumption always goes to the team that speaks last."
- [ ] "Presumption only applies to topicality, so it does not matter here."
- [ ] "The judge must vote aff whenever the neg runs a counterplan."

**Why:** When the neg goes for a counterplan, it is also asking for a change. The aff can argue that presumption goes to the side that asks for less change. Judges differ here. Some judges agree that presumption shifts. Others keep neg presumption, because the aff still has the burden of proof. That is why the teams should argue it.

## Order of steps

You sit down to decide a round. You are not sure yet if presumption matters.

What should you check first?

- [x] Whether either team has a reason to vote for it that survived the round
- [ ] Which team said the word "presumption" more often
- [ ] Your own default for presumption
- [ ] Which team asked for less change

**Why:** First check for offense. If either team has some, weigh it, and presumption does not come into play. Only if no one has offense do you look at the presumption arguments, and then at your default if no one argued it.
