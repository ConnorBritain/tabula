## Spot the turn

The neg reads a disadvantage (DA): an argument that the plan causes something bad. In the block (the 2NC and 1NR), the neg says this. "L" means link and "I" means impact.

```
DA: Jobs
L: plan's tax -> firms cut jobs
I: job losses -> less tax money
Turns case: less tax money ->
  plan's school program loses
  its funds (Analyst 21)
```

In words: the plan's tax makes firms cut jobs. Job losses mean less tax money. The neg then says less tax money takes away the funds the plan's school program needs.

What kind of turns case argument is the last line?

- [ ] An impact turns case: the DA makes the aff's harm worse
- [x] A solvency turns case: the DA's impact stops the plan from working
- [ ] A case turn: an on-case argument that the plan makes the harm worse
- [ ] Not a turn at all: it only says the DA is big

**Why:** The last line says the DA's impact cuts the money the plan needs, so the plan cannot work. That is solvency turns case. An impact turns case would say the DA makes the aff's harm itself worse.

## Turns case or case turn?

The names are close. Here are two neg arguments.

Which one is a turns case argument?

- [x] On the DA sheet: "The recession from our DA makes the poverty the aff wants to fix worse."
- [ ] On the case sheet: "The plan itself makes poverty worse, because it raises prices for poor families."

**Why:** Turns case is a weighing argument made from another sheet, usually a DA or a kritik: our impact makes your harm worse. A case turn is made on the case sheet and is about the plan itself. Many judges want a turns case argument cross-applied (carried over) to the case sheet, so the link is clear.

## What the turn does

The neg wins its DA. In the block, it said the DA turns the case, with a specific link. The aff never answered the turn.

What does the turn do to the weighing?

- [ ] Nothing, because the judge only compares the size of the two impacts
- [x] The aff's advantage shrinks, and the neg still has its DA impact
- [ ] The neg loses its DA impact, because it used the DA to turn the case
- [ ] The aff wins, because the turn is about the aff's case

**Why:** If the turn stands, the DA makes the aff's harm worse or stops the plan. So less of the advantage is left, and the neg keeps its own impact too. That is why a dropped turn, explained with a reason, is strong. The judge settles the turn first, then compares what is left.

## When the DA falls

The neg says its DA turns the case. But you decide the aff won "no link": the plan does not cause the DA at all.

What happens to the turns case argument?

- [x] It falls with the DA, because the turn depends on the DA happening
- [ ] It still counts in full, because the aff did not answer the turn itself
- [ ] It becomes an aff argument
- [ ] It counts for half, because the neg made it

**Why:** A turn is only as strong as the DA's chance of happening. If the plan does not cause the DA, the DA's impact does not happen, and it cannot hurt the plan. Attacking the DA's link is one way to answer a turn.
