## What should the 2NR go for?

The neg ran a DA (disadvantage), a counterplan (CP) and T (topicality) in the 1NC. Here is what the 1AR did on each.

```
1AR on T:
  We meet: plan text and
  card, explained well
1AR on the CP:
  Perm do both, explained;
  big solvency deficit
1AR on the DA:
  (no answer to the link or
  the uniqueness)
  "Case outweighs" (1 line)
```

In words: the 1AR answered T and the counterplan well. On the DA, it dropped the link and the uniqueness, and said only one line: "case outweighs."

What should the 2NR go for?

- [x] The DA: extend the dropped link and uniqueness, and weigh the DA against the case
- [ ] All three arguments, with the same time on each
- [ ] T alone, because T is a different question from the case
- [ ] The counterplan alone, because counterplans always beat a perm

**Why:** Look at what the 1AR answered badly or dropped. That is often the best place to go. The DA has dropped parts and only one thin line of weighing against it. Going for everything usually means winning nothing. The 1AR answered T and the counterplan well, so those are harder to win.

## Do the pieces fit?

The neg's counterplan: the states fund new buses. The neg's DA says any new government spending, by any level of government, forces cuts to schools.

Why is "the counterplan plus this DA" a weak choice for the 2NR?

- [ ] Because a counterplan can never have a DA as its net benefit
- [x] Because the counterplan also spends money, so it links to the DA too, and the DA gives no reason to prefer the counterplan
- [ ] Because the 2NR may go for only one argument
- [ ] Because the states cannot fund buses

**Why:** The net benefit must be a reason to prefer the counterplan to the plan. Here the states also spend money, so the counterplan causes the same cuts. A counterplan with a net benefit that the counterplan also links to is a weak pair.

## Dropping an argument safely

The 2NR wants to stop going for a DA. But in the 1AR, the aff extended a link turn on that DA: "The plan actually stops the DA's harm."

What should the 2NR do?

- [ ] Say nothing about the DA, because a dropped DA is out of the round
- [x] Answer the link turn, or explain why it does not matter, before it drops the DA
- [ ] Extend the DA's impact card one more time
- [ ] Make a new DA in its place

**Why:** A link turn is aff offense: a reason to vote aff. If the 2NR drops the DA and ignores the turn, the aff can still win on it. So answer the turn, or explain why it does not matter. Many teams also say out loud: "We are not going for the DA."

## Build the 2AR

The 2NR went for one DA and nothing else. It dropped T. In the 1AR, the aff said "no link" on the DA and "case outweighs."

What should the 2AR do? Pick all that apply.

- [x] Extend the 1AR's "no link" answer and explain it
- [x] Weigh with "even if": even if the DA is true, the case outweighs
- [ ] Spend time on T, to be safe
- [ ] Add a new impact turn that was not in the 1AR

**Why:** Start where the 2NR went: the DA. Use the 1AR's answers, and weigh with "even if," so you are covered if the judge does not buy "no link." The neg dropped T, so do not spend time there. Most judges ignore brand-new arguments in the 2AR.
