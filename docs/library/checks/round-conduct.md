## A card skipped "not"

Between speeches, a neg debater tells you: "Their card (Writer 20) leaves out the word 'not.' That changes what the author meant." You do not know this league's rule for evidence challenges.

What is the right step?

- [ ] Give the round to the neg at once, because changing a quote is cheating
- [ ] Tell the neg to stop interrupting and go on with the round
- [x] Check the tournament's rules for evidence challenges, or ask the tab room (the tournament's central office), before you decide
- [ ] Make up a fair rule on the spot and tell both teams

**Why:** Leagues handle evidence challenges in different ways. Do not invent your own rule. Read the tournament's rules, or stop the round and ask the tab room. In some leagues a formal challenge ends the round. In others, the team debates it in a speech.

## Weak card or ethics?

In the 1AR, the aff says: "Their card is from many years ago, and the tag says more than the card does. That is an ethics violation."

What is this most likely?

- [x] A matter for debate: the neg can answer it, and the judge weighs it
- [ ] An ethics problem that should stop the round
- [ ] A safety problem to report to tournament staff
- [ ] Clipping

**Why:** Not every weak card is an ethics problem. An old card, or a tag that overstates the card, is usually a point to debate. An ethics problem is about honesty: a made-up quote, changed or cut words that change the meaning, clipping, or a false cite.

## When to raise it

During the 2AC, a neg debater sees that a card in the speech doc is missing a line.

When should the neg raise it?

- [ ] At once, by stopping the 2AC in the middle
- [x] Between speeches, calmly, with the judge
- [ ] Only after the ballot is in
- [ ] Never, because the judge should find it alone

**Why:** A debater who sees a problem usually tells the judge between speeches, not in the middle of a speech. Then the judge can follow the tournament's procedure.

## What counts as proof

A team makes a formal evidence challenge. You must decide whether the violation happened.

What can help you decide? Pick all that apply.

- [x] The original source the card came from
- [x] The speech doc (the file of cards the team shared)
- [x] A recording of the speech, where the tournament allows one
- [ ] How sure the challenging team sounds

**Why:** A judge needs proof of the problem: the original source, the speech doc, or a recording where one is allowed. How confident a team sounds is not proof. A clipping claim is hard to prove without a recording.

## You see it yourself

No one raises anything. But you see a debater clearly skip highlighted words while saying they read the whole card.

What is the right step?

- [ ] Vote against that team, and say nothing to anyone
- [ ] Ignore it, because no one raised it
- [x] Follow the tournament's procedure, which often means contacting the tab room
- [ ] Ask the other team to make it an argument in their next speech

**Why:** Skipping words while claiming to read them is clipping. If a judge sees a problem no one raised, the judge follows the tournament's procedure. Many tournaments want judges to contact the tab room, not act alone.
