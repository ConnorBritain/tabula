## Set up a DA

You are on the neg. In the 1NC, your partner will read a DA (disadvantage) that says the plan costs a lot of money and forces cuts. Now you cross-ex the 1AC.

Which question best sets up that DA?

- [x] "Yes or no: does your plan cost money?"
- [ ] "Can you explain your whole case again?"
- [ ] "Don't you agree your plan is a bad idea?"
- [ ] "Why did your team pick this case?"

**Why:** A closed question gets a commitment you can use. If they say yes, the 1NC can say: "In cross-ex, they said the plan costs money." The open question only repeats the case. The third question argues, and you should save arguments for your speech. The last question has no goal.

## Set up an evidence attack

The 1AC's harm card (a quote used as evidence) says no program helps rural clinics. You know a new program for rural clinics started last year. You want to argue in your speech that their card is out of date.

Which cross-ex question best sets up that argument?

- [ ] "Is your card a good card?"
- [x] "What year is your harm card from?"
- [ ] "Isn't it true your whole case is wrong?"
- [ ] "Can I see all of your cards at the end of cross-ex?"

**Why:** Know which speech will use each answer. If the card is from before the new program, you can say so in your speech. Do not argue in cross-ex: get the answer, then make the argument later. Ask for cards at the start of cross-ex, not at the end.

## Answer without giving a link

You are on the aff. In cross-ex, the neg asks: "Does your plan need new workers?"

Which answer is best?

- [x] "Yes. The plan hires new staff in its first year."
- [ ] "Yes, and that might take workers away from hospitals."
- [ ] "I won't answer that."
- [ ] "Yes, but let me give you a speech about why that is good."

**Why:** Answer the question asked, short and honest. Do not concede (admit) more than you must. The second answer gives the neg a link: proof that the plan causes a bad result. Refusing looks evasive and costs points. Cross-ex is not a speech, so save your arguments for your partner's next speech.

## Does it count?

In cross-ex of the 2AC, the aff said: "The plan has no way to pay for itself." The neg never mentioned this answer in any later speech.

How do most judges treat that cross-ex answer when they decide?

- [ ] As a winning argument for the neg
- [x] As not part of the decision, because no one used it in a speech
- [ ] As a reason to lower the aff's speaker points only
- [ ] As a new argument the aff must answer in the 1AR

**Why:** Cross-ex is not a speech. An answer counts for the decision only if a team uses it in a speech. The neg should have said in a later speech: "In cross-ex, they said the plan has no way to pay."
