## Pick the best RFD

The last two speeches were about one disadvantage. The 2NR said the plan's cost forces cuts to school programs. The 2AR said the plan saves money by year 3. The neg never answered the year-3 point. You vote aff.

Which RFD best explains the decision?

- [ ] "I vote aff. Good job, both teams."
- [x] "I vote aff. The round came down to the plan's cost. The neg never answered that the plan saves money by year 3, so the link to the disadvantage is turned. If the 2NR had explained why the cuts come before the savings, I might have voted neg."
- [ ] "I vote aff because I think the plan is a good idea in real life."

**Why:** A good RFD names the decision, the main issue, why that team won it, and what could have changed it. "Good job" says nothing about the arguments. Your own view of the plan should not decide the round.

## Where to start

You sit down to decide. Your flow has eight speeches on it.

Where should you start?

- [ ] At the 1AC, and read every speech in order
- [x] At the 2NR and the 2AR, and list the issues each one said were most important
- [ ] At the cross-ex notes
- [ ] At the speech with the most cards

**Why:** Start at the end. The last two speeches tell you what each team is going for. Then read each issue back across your flow to see who won it. Arguments that neither team kept in the last speeches are usually gone.

## No weighing

Each team won one issue. Neither team compared the two impacts in the last speeches.

What should you do?

- [ ] Flip a coin
- [ ] Make new arguments for the team you prefer
- [x] Do your best to compare, and say in your RFD that more comparison would have helped
- [ ] Give the round to the team that spoke last

**Why:** Do not make arguments for a team. But you still must decide. Compare as fairly as you can from what was said, and tell the debaters that comparing impacts would have helped them.

## Write a short RFD

You flow this at the end of a round.

```
2NR                          2AR
T: plan is not "substantial" We meet: plan changes 12%,
($ change is only 2%)        above their 10% line
                             (2NR never answered 12%)
```

In words: the neg said the plan is not topical, because it changes funding by only 2 percent. The aff said the plan changes funding by 12 percent, which is above the neg's own 10 percent line. The 2NR never answered the 12 percent point.

Write a two-sentence RFD for this round in the box. Then compare it with the checklist.

**Self-check:**

- [ ] Did your RFD say who won?
- [ ] Did it name the main issue (topicality, and whether the plan meets the neg's own line)?
- [ ] Did it say why that team won it (the 2NR never answered the 12 percent point)?

**Model answer:** I vote aff. The neg's topicality argument says the plan must change funding by at least 10 percent, and the aff showed the plan changes it by 12 percent, which the 2NR never answered.

**Why:** A short RFD can still name the decision, the issue and the reason. If you have room, add what would have changed your mind, for example: "If the neg had shown the 12 percent card was wrong, I might have voted neg."
