## What the 2AR may do

The 2NR has ended. The second aff speaker stands up to give the 2AR, the last speech of the round.

Which of these may the 2AR do? Pick all that apply.

- [x] Answer the 2NR, with new explanation of the aff's arguments
- [x] Extend an answer that the 1AR made
- [x] Tell the judge how to weigh the arguments
- [ ] Read a brand-new advantage that no aff speech said before

**Why:** The 2AR answers the 2NR, extends the aff's best path to win, and tells the judge how to weigh. It can explain old arguments in new words. It cannot add brand-new arguments, because the neg has no speech left to answer them. Most judges ignore a brand-new 2AR argument.

## A missing answer in the 2AC

The 1NC read three off-case arguments: a topicality argument, a DA (disadvantage) and a counterplan. It also read answers on the case.

```
1NC          2AC
T            answered
DA           (no answer)
CP           answered
Case         answered
```

In words: the 2AC answered the topicality argument, the counterplan and the case. It said nothing about the DA.

What is the problem with this 2AC?

- [x] It dropped the DA, and a dropped argument is hard to bring back later
- [ ] There is no problem, because the 1AR can answer the DA for the first time
- [ ] It should have answered only one argument and dropped the rest
- [ ] There is no problem, because a DA is not on the case sheet

**Why:** The 2AC must answer every argument from the 1NC, even briefly. An argument the aff does not answer is called a drop. A dropped argument is hard to bring back, so the DA now helps the neg.

## Splitting the block

The 2NC and the 1NR come back to back. Together they are the negative block. In the 2NC, the second neg speaker answers the 2AC on the DA and the counterplan.

What should the first neg speaker do in the 1NR?

- [ ] Answer the DA and the counterplan again, to make them stronger
- [x] Take the other arguments, such as topicality and the case
- [ ] Read new off-case arguments that were not in the 1NC
- [ ] Say nothing, because the 2NC already spoke

**Why:** The two block speakers split the arguments. They should not both answer the same arguments. If the 2NC took the DA and the counterplan, the 1NR takes the rest, such as topicality and the case.

## The last neg speech

It is time for the 2NR, the neg's last speech. The neg still has four arguments that it could go for: topicality, a DA, a counterplan and case answers. The judge has not decided yet.

What should a good 2NR do?

- [ ] Go for all four arguments, a little time on each
- [x] Choose one or two arguments, and explain why they win and outweigh the case
- [ ] Read new arguments, because it is the neg's last chance
- [ ] Repeat the 1NC word for word

**Why:** The 2NR should choose. Most good 2NRs go for one or two arguments, not everything. It explains why those arguments win and why they outweigh the case. New arguments are not allowed in the 2NR, except to answer new things from the 1AR.
