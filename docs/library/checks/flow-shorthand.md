## Arrows and reasons

You see this line on your flow: `plan -> ↓ jobs b/c $`

What does it mean?

- [x] The plan leads to fewer jobs, because of money or cost
- [ ] The plan leads to more jobs, with money
- [ ] Jobs cause the plan, because of the economy
- [ ] The plan is the same as fewer jobs, without money

**Why:** `->` means "causes" or "leads to". `↓` means decrease, so `↓ jobs` is fewer jobs. `b/c` means "because", and `$` means money, cost or the economy.

## Not the same

Your partner wrote this line during the 2AC: `SQ ≠ solve w/o $`

What does it mean?

- [ ] The status quo solves, with money
- [x] The status quo does not solve without money
- [ ] The plan solves faster than the status quo
- [ ] Solvency is unclear, and no warrant was given

**Why:** `SQ` is the status quo, the world now. `≠` means "is not" or "does not"; here, "does not solve". `w/o` means "without", and `$` is money. So the line says the world now does not fix the problem without money.

## Which way it weighs

In the 2AR column, you see: `Case O/W DA`

What does it mean?

- [x] The case outweighs the DA
- [ ] The DA outweighs the case
- [ ] The case and the DA are the same
- [ ] The DA was dropped

**Why:** `O/W` means "outweighs". Read it left to right: the case outweighs the DA. If the neg says the DA is bigger, you might see `DA O/W case` or `DA > case`.

## The star

On a DA sheet, you see a star next to one part: `IL: ↑ $ -> cuts *`

On the list on this page, what does a star mean?

- [ ] The part is the most important argument in the round
- [x] The part was dropped: the other team did not answer it
- [ ] The part has no evidence, and the reason is unclear
- [ ] The part is a turn

**Why:** A star (or a circle) marks an argument that was dropped, or not answered. Mark every drop the same way, so you can find it fast in the rebuttals. A `?` is the symbol for an unclear argument with no warrant.
