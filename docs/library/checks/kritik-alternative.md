## What the alt asks

The aff's advantage describes a border as "a war zone" that must be secured. The neg's alternative says: "Vote neg to reject the aff's description of the border as a war zone."

What does this alternative ask the judge to do?

- [ ] Pass a different law about the border
- [x] Reject the aff's way of describing the problem
- [ ] Do the plan, but more slowly
- [ ] Vote for whichever team spoke more clearly

**Why:** This is a "reject" alt. It asks the judge to reject the aff's way of thinking, here its framing of the border. It does not propose a new law. Other alts ask the judge to rethink the problem first, or support a different action by a different actor.

## Explain the solvency

The neg's kritik says the aff describes poor families as a burden, and that framing leads to harsh policies. The alt is to reject that framing.

Which neg line explains how the alt solves the kritik's impact?

- [ ] "The alt solves."
- [ ] "The alt is in the 1NC, so it counts."
- [x] "When the judge rejects the 'burden' framing, the next policy must start from what families need, so the harsh policies stop repeating."
- [ ] "The aff did not read a card against the alt."

**Why:** Alt solvency is what the alt achieves. The neg should explain *how* the alt reduces the kritik's harm. "The alt solves" is a claim with no explanation. The right choice says what changes and why that reduces the harm.

## Kicking the alt

In the 2NR, the neg says: "We kick the alt. The links are a disadvantage: the aff's threat talk leads to conflict."

What is the neg asking the judge to compare now?

- [ ] The alt against the perm
- [x] The aff's case against the present system, plus the risk from the kritik's links
- [ ] Nothing, because a kritik with no alt is gone
- [ ] The alt against the present system

**Why:** With the alt kicked, there is no alt left to compare or to perm. The neg asks the judge to treat the links like a disadvantage and weigh that risk against the case. The aff often answers that the present system thinks the same way, so the link is not unique. Some judges treat a kritik without an alt as a disadvantage. Others accept that a won framework makes the link enough.

## The alt changes shape

The 1NC alt was "reject the aff's frame." In the 2NR, the neg explains it as: "Do the plan, but without the aff's threat framing."

What has the alt become?

- [ ] A link turn
- [x] A PIK: an alt that keeps the plan but rejects part of the aff
- [ ] A permutation
- [ ] A role of the ballot

**Why:** A PIK (plan-inclusive kritik) keeps the plan and rejects some part of the aff, often its words or reasons. When a PIK appears late, from the wording of the alt, it is called a floating PIK. Judges vary: many are skeptical of one that first shows up in the 2NR, and others accept it if the 1NC words fairly include it. The aff can ask in cross-ex: "Does the alt let us do the plan?" A perm comes from the aff. Here the neg is explaining its own alternative, so it is a PIK, not a perm.
