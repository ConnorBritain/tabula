## Who is ahead on the link? (1)

The aff's plan pays private builders to make low-cost homes. The neg reads a capitalism kritik, or cap K, which says the plan relies on market thinking.

Names in parentheses are made-up cards.

```
LINK
1NC: Plan uses profit to
     house people (Analyst 23).
2AC: No link: plan is public
     spending.
2NR: Plan pays builders for
     profit; CX said so.
2AR: (not extended)
```

In words: the 1NC says the plan uses profit to house people. The 2AC says there is no link, because the plan is public spending. The 2NR says the plan pays builders to make a profit, and cites a cross-ex answer. The 2AR does not extend the no-link answer.

Which side is ahead on the link?

- [x] The neg, because the 2AR let the no-link answer go and the 2NR had a specific link
- [ ] The aff, because the 2AC said "no link" first
- [ ] Neither, because a link cannot be won without the alt
- [ ] The aff, because it spoke last

**Why:** The 2NR applied the link to this plan's tool, with a cross-ex answer. The 2AR did not extend "no link," so the neg's link stands. Being ahead on the link does not decide the round by itself. The judge still weighs framework, the impact and the case.

## Who is ahead on the link? (2)

A different round. The aff's plan has a public agency build homes and give them free to families in need.

Names in parentheses are made-up cards.

```
LINK
1NC: All state action props
     up the market (Analyst 20).
2AC: 1. Generic: links to any
     aff.
     2. Link turn: plan takes
     homes out of the market.
2NR: Every plan is capitalist.
2AR: Ext turn: dropped by 2NR.
```

In words: the 1NC says all state action supports the market. The 2AC says the link is generic, and it makes a link turn: the plan takes homes out of the market. The 2NR repeats that every plan is capitalist, but does not answer the link turn. The 2AR extends the turn and says the 2NR dropped it.

Which side is ahead on the link?

- [ ] The neg, because "every plan is capitalist" covers this plan too
- [x] The aff, because the neg's link is generic and the 2NR never answered the link turn
- [ ] Neither, because both teams talked about the link
- [ ] The neg, because the 1NC read a card and the 2AC did not

**Why:** The neg's link fits any aff, and the 2NR only repeated it. The 2AC's link turn went unanswered, and the 2AR extended it. So the aff is ahead on the link. A link to this plan's mechanism would have helped the neg more than "all policies."

## Cap good and a perm

The 2AC says: "Cap is good: growth cuts poverty (Analyst 19)." It also says: "Perm: do the plan and the alt." The alt rejects market logic.

Names in parentheses are made-up cards.

What will the neg likely argue about these two answers?

- [x] They contradict: the aff cannot say capitalism is good and also do an alt that rejects it
- [ ] They prove the aff has no link
- [ ] The perm wins the round for the aff
- [ ] They mean the neg must drop the kritik

**Why:** An impact turn and a perm do not mix well. If the aff says capitalism is good, it is hard to also say it will do an alt that rejects capitalism. The aff can choose one story by the 1AR, or explain why the two do not conflict.

## What "cap good" claims

The 2AR says: "Even if ideas count, cap is good. Market economies cut poverty and drove new technology."

What is the aff claiming with this argument?

- [ ] That the harms the kritik names are good
- [x] That the economic system the kritik criticizes does more good than harm
- [ ] That the plan does not use markets
- [ ] That the alt is too vague

**Why:** "Cap good" is an impact turn: it says the system the neg calls bad does more good than harm. It defends an economic system, not the harms the kritik names. Judges differ on it. Some find it a strong, honest answer. Others think it is hard to win against a well-evidenced kritik. Decide on the evidence and explanation in the round.
