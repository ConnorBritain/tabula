## A note for the 2AR

The round came down to one disadvantage. In the 2AR (the last aff speech), the aff never compared its impact with the neg's impact.

Which note to the 2AR is best?

- [ ] "Good job. Keep it up."
- [ ] "You were lazy in the 2AR."
- [x] "In the 2AR, you did not compare the two impacts. Next time, say out loud why yours is bigger or more likely."
- [ ] "Your outfit was not right for a tournament."

**Why:** Good feedback is specific and kind, and gives a next step. It ties advice to a moment in the round. "Good job" teaches nothing. "Lazy" is about the person, not the speech. Never comment on looks or clothing.

## Something to keep

The 1NC (the first neg speech) read a disadvantage with a link card that named the plan directly. It was the strongest part of the speech.

Which comment best tells the 1N (the first neg speaker) what to keep doing?

- [ ] "Nice speech."
- [x] "Your link card named the plan directly, so the disadvantage was easy to follow. Keep finding cards that specific."
- [ ] "Better than the other team."
- [ ] "You talk well."

**Why:** Specific beats general. A comment that names what worked, and why, shows the debater what to repeat. General praise feels nice but teaches nothing.

## No disclosure

The tournament asks judges not to disclose (tell the teams who won) in this round. There is time for an oral critique.

What can you say?

- [ ] Hint at the winner with your face and tone
- [ ] Tell only the winning team, quietly, after the round
- [x] Give each debater specific advice, and do not say who won
- [ ] Say nothing at all, because you cannot disclose

**Why:** If you may not disclose, skip the decision and the reason. You can still give each debater specific advice and stop.

## The right order

The tournament lets you disclose. Your ballot is in.

In what order should you give your oral critique?

- [x] The decision, then the reason, then what would have changed your mind, then notes for each debater
- [ ] Notes for each debater, then a long review of every speech, then the decision at the end
- [ ] The decision, then nothing else
- [ ] Notes for the losing team only, then the decision

**Why:** Give the decision first, so the teams do not wait through a long speech. Then the reason and what would have changed your mind: that part helps the team that lost most. Then give each debater, on both teams, one or two specific notes.
