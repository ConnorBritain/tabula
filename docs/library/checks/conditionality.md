## What "conditional" means

In cross-examination, the aff asks: "What is the status of the counterplan?" The neg says: "Conditional."

What did the neg just tell the aff?

- [ ] The neg must keep the counterplan to the end of the round
- [x] The neg may drop the counterplan at any time, for any reason
- [ ] The neg may drop the counterplan only if the aff makes a permutation
- [ ] The judge will decide later if the counterplan counts

**Why:** A conditional option is one the neg may drop at any time, for any reason. An unconditional option must stay to the end. An option the neg may drop only under set conditions, such as a perm, is dispositional.

## After the kick

The 1NC reads a counterplan, a disadvantage (DA) against the plan, and case arguments. The counterplan is conditional. In the 2NR, the neg says: "We kick the counterplan. Vote neg on the DA."

What does the judge compare now?

- [ ] The counterplan with the status quo
- [x] The plan with the status quo, with the DA still in the round
- [ ] Nothing, because the neg dropped its own argument, so the aff wins
- [ ] The plan with the counterplan, because the 1NC read it

**Why:** When the neg kicks (drops) a counterplan, the counterplan leaves the round. The judge compares the plan with the status quo: the world as it is now. The neg can still win with the DA and case arguments it extended.

## A different status

In cross-examination, the neg says: "We can drop the counterplan only if you make a permutation."

Which status is this, in the most common use of the word?

- [ ] Unconditional
- [ ] Conditional
- [x] Dispositional

**Why:** Dispositional ("dispo") means the neg may drop the option only under set conditions. A common condition is that the aff makes a permutation (a test that does both plans). Definitions of dispo vary between teams, so the aff should ask what the neg means.

## What goes with it?

The neg ran a conditional counterplan and a DA against the plan. The aff answered the counterplan with a DA against it. In cross-examination, the neg admitted that the link card on its DA against the plan is about a different program. In the 2NR, the neg kicks the counterplan and goes for the DA.

What can the aff still use after the kick? Pick all that apply.

- [ ] The DA against the counterplan
- [x] The neg's admission in cross-examination about its DA's link card
- [x] Its answers to the neg's arguments against the plan

**Why:** Kicking removes the counterplan, so the DA against the counterplan goes away with it. The neg's arguments against the plan are still in the round, so the aff's answers to them still count. The cross-examination admission is about the DA against the plan, which is still in the round, so the aff can still use it: the link card is about a different program.
