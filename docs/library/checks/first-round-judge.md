## Keeping time

Before the round, a debater asks: "Will you keep time for us too?"

What should you do?

- [x] Say yes, and time the speeches and prep on your watch or phone
- [ ] Say no, because only debaters keep time
- [ ] Say yes, but time only the first speech
- [ ] Ask the other team to decide for you

**Why:** Debaters usually time themselves, but the judge should time too. It is a normal request.

## Something new in the last speech

The neg says the plan costs too much money. In its earlier speeches, the aff only said "the plan costs nothing." Then, in the 2AR (the last speech of the round), the aff says for the first time: "The plan actually saves money."

How do most judges treat this new point?

- [ ] Count it, because the 2AR is the last speech
- [x] Not count it, because it is new, and the neg had no speech left to answer it
- [ ] Count it only if the aff reads evidence for it
- [ ] Give the round to the neg, because new arguments are cheating

**Why:** The point was not in the aff's earlier speeches, so it is new in the 2AR. The neg has no speech left to answer it. Most judges do not count it. Some judges want the neg to point this out, but most will not count it either way. It does not end the round by itself.

## What to vote on

At the end of a round, you think about how to decide.

Which of these should decide your vote? Pick all that apply.

- [x] Which team won the most important issue in the last two speeches
- [x] Which team explained its side with better reasons
- [ ] Whether you agree with the plan in real life
- [ ] Which team you liked more as people

**Why:** Decide only from what the debaters said. Look at the main fight in the last two speeches, and at who explained it better. Your own view of the policy, and how much you liked a team, should not count.

## If you are confused

You did not understand how the counterplan was different from the plan. The aff explained the plan's benefits clearly.

What is a good thing to do?

- [ ] Vote for the team that read the most evidence, and do not mention it
- [x] Decide from your notes, and say kindly in your RFD that you did not understand the counterplan
- [ ] Refuse to sign the ballot
- [ ] Ask the debaters after the round which team should win

**Why:** Decide from what you understood. Saying so kindly in the RFD is useful feedback, because it teaches the debaters to explain better.
