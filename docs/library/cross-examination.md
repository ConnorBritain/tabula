# Cross-examination (CX)

> **Plain version.** After each constructive speech, the other team asks questions for three minutes. This is cross-examination, or cross-ex. It is a chance to understand the other team's arguments, to pin down what they will defend, and to set up your next speech. Cross-ex is not a speech. An answer in cross-ex matters for the decision only if someone uses it in a speech. Judges do listen closely, and good cross-ex often decides how a round goes.

## What cross-ex is for

There are three main jobs:

1. **Clarify.** "What does your plan do?" "Which card says that?" You cannot answer an argument you do not understand.
2. **Commit.** Get the other team to say what they will and will not defend. "Does your plan cost money?" "Is your counterplan conditional?"
3. **Set up.** Get an answer you will use in your next speech. "So your evidence is from before the new law passed?"

A fourth job is quieter: **show the judge you understand the round.** Calm, sharp questions earn speaker points.

## How to ask good questions

- **Ask short questions.** One idea per question. Long questions get long answers.
- **Ask closed questions when you want a commitment.** "Yes or no: does the plan take effect this year?" Use open questions only when you want to learn something.
- **Have a goal for each question.** Know which speech will use the answer.
- **Do not argue.** If the answer is weak, say "Thank you" and move on. Make the argument in your speech.
- **Control the time politely.** If an answer runs long, say "Thank you, that is enough." It is your time.
- **Ask for documents and cards at the start**, not halfway through.

## How to answer well

- **Answer the question asked.** Short, honest answers look confident.
- **Do not guess.** If you do not know, say so, or say "My partner will explain that in the next speech."
- **Do not concede more than you must.** "The plan does X" is fine. "The plan does X and that might cause Y" gives the other team a link.
- **Ask for the question again** if it is unclear. "Can you say that again?" is better than answering the wrong question.
- **Stay calm and polite.** Judges notice tone. Rude or evasive answers cost points.

## Who asks and who answers

Usually, the debater who just spoke answers, and the debater on the other team who does *not* speak next asks. So:

| Cross-ex of | Asks | Answers |
|---|---|---|
| 1AC | 2nd neg | 1st aff |
| 1NC | 1st aff | 1st neg |
| 2AC | 1st neg | 2nd aff |
| 2NC | 2nd aff | 2nd neg |

Some leagues allow **open cross-ex**, where either partner can ask or answer, or **tag-team** help. Others do not. Check the tournament's rules.

**Prep time** is sometimes used as extra cross-ex time, by agreement. Teams may ask questions during the other team's prep. This varies by league and by judge.

## How judges use cross-ex

- **Flow key answers in the margin.** Write "CX: plan costs $5B" next to the related argument.
- **Count an answer only if it is used in a speech.** Cross-ex alone does not win an argument.
- **Watch for commitments.** If a team says "the CP is not conditional" in cross-ex, many judges hold them to it.
- **Reward good cross-ex in speaker points.** Clear questions and clear answers show skill.

## Example in flow shorthand

A 1NC cross-ex that sets up a disadvantage, and how it shows up on the flow later.

```
CX of 1AC (margin note)       1NC                      2AC                    Block
CX: plan costs $ "some"       DA: Spending             1. No L: plan is       Ext L: 1AC said in
    but no number given       L: plan costs $50B          cost-neutral         CX it costs $; no
CX: funded by "normal            (Expert 20)                                   offset named
    means" -> no offset
```

*How to read it:* in cross-ex, the aff admitted the plan costs money and named no way to pay for it. The 1NC read a spending link. When the 2AC said "no link," the block used the cross-ex answers to show the link.

## Common mistakes

- **Making speeches in cross-ex.** Fix: ask, get the answer, and save the argument for your speech.
- **Asking questions with no goal.** Fix: before cross-ex, write 3 or 4 questions tied to your next speech.
- **Never using the answers.** Fix: refer to the cross-ex in your speech: "In cross-ex, they said..."
- **Answering with "I do not know" too often.** Fix: know your own case. If you truly do not know, point to your partner's next speech.
- **Being rude or talking over the other debater.** Fix: be firm but polite. It is a conversation, not a fight.
- **Judges treating a cross-ex answer as a full argument.** Fix: it counts only if a team uses it in a speech.
