# Debate Library: who reads each page

Status: v1, 2026-11-20. Owner rule: write each page for the reader who most likely lands on it, at the point in their season when they land on it. Use the words that reader already knows. Explain the rest. This file is the source for that reader. `PLAN.md` §5 (tone) still applies, and so does its rule that every page can be the first page a reader sees: explain each term the first time it shows on a page.

## The five readers

| Id | Reader | When and how they arrive | What they already know | What they need |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **J1** | **New parent judge.** Adult, no debate background. | The night before or the morning of their first tournament. On a phone, 5 to 8 minutes at a time. Sent by a coach, or from a search. | Nothing. Not "aff" or "neg", not the speech names, not what a flow is. | To judge one fair round today. Plain words, the steps in the order they happen, and calm. |
| **J2** | **Parent judge, rounds 2 to 10.** | Between rounds or between tournaments, after a round that confused them. Phone or laptop. | Aff and neg, the plan, that there are 8 speeches and cross-ex, rough notes in columns, the ballot and RFD. A few words they heard but cannot define (DA, counterplan, kritik, "extend"). | To decide better and explain it. Clear rules of thumb, and what the words they keep hearing mean. |
| **S1** | **Novice debater, first month.** About 14. | Assigned by a coach before practice, or searching the night before a first tournament. Laptop or phone. | What a round looks like from one or two practices. Aff and neg, the plan, the speech names (not yet the times). Not yet: cards, stock issues, DA parts. | To do the thing at the next practice or round. One idea at a time, a worked example, what to say out loud. |
| **S2** | **Novice or JV debater, months 2 to 5.** | After losing on an argument, or when a coach assigns the next topic. Laptop. | Speeches and times, flowing, cards, the stock issues, the four parts of a DA, what a counterplan and topicality are. Has heard "kritik", "perm", "theory" in rounds. | To run and answer the argument well. The parts named, how they connect, and how judges decide it. |
| **S3** | **Varsity debater, coach, or experienced judge.** | Looking for a clean, fair statement to check their thinking or to send to a novice. Laptop. | The standard vocabulary. Most theory and kritik terms. | Precision, fairness to every style, and nothing wrong. They do not need hand-holding, but a novice they send the page to does: keep first-use definitions short. |

## How to test a page (the attention test)

Two readers test each page:

1. **The arriving reader** (the page's primary reader above). Read once, top to bottom, at their speed and on their device. Log each section as `in`, `drifting`, `skimming` or `gone`, with the line where it changed. Mark every term they would not know at that point, and whether the page explains it before or after it is used.
2. **The pacing reader.** Read it aloud in your head. Rate each section's energy 1 to 5. Mark where a listener would skip: repeats, long runs of same-shape sentences, a section longer than its payload, a point made more than once.

Score each reader 0 to 10 (8 = finished without skimming; 9 = would act on it). A page passes at 8 or more from both.

**Right-sizing rules** (what "right level" means):
- Reading level stays as `PLAN.md` §5 sets it: grade 7 to 8 for the Plain version, grade 9 to 10 for the rest.
- Explain every term the primary reader would not know, in plain words, the first time it is used and before it matters. Plain word first, term after: "the aff (affirmative)".
- Every term still gets a first-use explanation (`PLAN.md` §5). For S2 and S3 readers, keep it to one short clause, so a novice who is sent the page can still follow without slowing the reader the page is for.
- Put things in the order the reader needs them.
- Say each point once. Keep repeats only in the Plain version and Common mistakes.
- Examples must read on a phone: flow lines of 40 characters or fewer, and a `Key:` line above the block for short forms (`PLAN.md` §4).
- Keep every fact, rule, number and "some judges think X; others think Y" balance. Cut words, not content.
