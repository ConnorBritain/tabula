# Uniqueness (U)

> **Plain version.** Uniqueness is the first part of a disadvantage. It says the bad thing is not happening now. That matters because a disadvantage must show the plan makes a difference. If the harm will happen anyway, the plan does not cause it, and the disadvantage is "non-unique." So the neg must prove the world is fine on this issue today, and the plan is what tips it over.

## What uniqueness is

A **disadvantage** (DA) is a neg argument that the plan causes something bad. It has four parts: uniqueness, link, internal link and impact. See [Disadvantages](disadvantages.md) for the whole chain.

Uniqueness is a claim about the **status quo**: the world as it is now, without the plan. It says, "On this issue, things are okay right now." For example: "The budget is balanced this year." Or: "The two countries are at peace now."

The DA then says the plan changes that. Uniqueness gives the DA its "before" picture. The link gives the "after."

## Why it matters

Think of a judge who compares two worlds: the world with the plan, and the world without it. A DA counts only if it is worse in the plan world. If the harm happens in both worlds, it is not a reason to reject the plan.

That is what **non-unique** (N/U) means. "Non-unique" is the aff's answer that the harm is already happening, or will happen soon anyway. If the aff wins it, the plan adds little or nothing to the harm.

## Uniqueness and the link: a scale

Uniqueness and the link work together, like weight on a scale.

| Uniqueness | Link needed |
|---|---|
| Very strong: "the budget is very safe" | A big link, to tip it |
| Weak: "the budget is right on the edge" | Even a small link may tip it |

Some debaters call the tipping point the **brink** or the **threshold**. When uniqueness says the world is right at the brink, the neg needs only a small push from the plan. When uniqueness says the world is far from the brink, the neg needs a large push.

This gives each side a way to argue. The neg wants uniqueness that says "close to the edge, but not over it." The aff can say "already over the edge" (non-unique) or "so far from the edge that the plan cannot push it over" (the link is too small).

## Uniqueness controls the direction of the link

This is the idea new debaters miss most often. Who wins uniqueness decides which side the link helps.

- **If the neg wins uniqueness** (the harm is not coming now), the link is neg offense. The plan starts the harm. A link turn then helps the aff less, because the plan only prevents a harm that was not coming.
- **If the aff wins non-unique** (the harm is coming anyway), the neg's link adds little. But a **link turn** (the plan does the opposite, and prevents the harm) becomes aff offense. The plan stops a harm that was on its way.

So "non-unique" and a link turn make a strong pair for the aff. One says the harm is coming. The other says the plan stops it. See [Links and link turns](links.md).

When the evidence on uniqueness is close, some debaters argue the reverse: "the link controls the direction of uniqueness." That means a clear, large link matters more than a close uniqueness fight. Judges decide this from what the teams argue.

## How to answer uniqueness

These answers are for the aff, usually in the 2AC (the second affirmative constructive).

- **Non-unique:** the harm is already happening. Read evidence that shows it.
- **Thumper:** something else in the world now should already cause the harm. For example, "a big new bill already spends billions." If that did not cause the harm, the plan will not either. If it did, the harm is already here.
- **Uniqueness overwhelms the link:** the status quo is so safe that the plan cannot change it.
- **Newer evidence:** the world changes. Show that the neg's evidence is out of date.

These answers are for the neg, usually in the block (the 2NC and 1NR).

- **Read newer or better uniqueness evidence.** Explain why it is more recent or more specific.
- **Answer each thumper.** Show why it is different from the plan, or why it is already over.
- **Say why the link is enough.** Even if the status quo is not perfect, the plan makes it much worse.

## How to judge uniqueness

1. **Compare two worlds.** Ask whether the harm is more likely with the plan than without it. That is the uniqueness question.
2. **Look at dates and detail.** Uniqueness is often about facts that change fast. Many judges give more weight to newer evidence on uniqueness, if a team points out the dates. Many judges do not do this work for a team that did not ask.
3. **Decide uniqueness before the link.** The answer tells you whether the link or the link turn is the offense.
4. **Do not demand perfect uniqueness.** "The harm is a bit likely now" does not end the DA. The plan can still raise the risk.

For uniqueness questions in common DA shapes, see [Common DA shapes](common-disadvantages.md).

## Example in flow shorthand

A generic spending DA. The middle speeches are left out to save space. Each part shows the speeches that argue it, top to bottom.

Key: N/U = non-unique. L/T = link turn. $ = money. -> = so. Names in parentheses are invented authors.

```
DA: SPENDING

UNIQUENESS
1NC: Budget at its cap, no room
     for new $ (Analyst 21)
2AC: 1. N/U: road bill adds
     $40B now (Author 22)
2NR: Road bill was paid for by
     cuts in same law -> no net
     new $ (Analyst 23)
2AR: Those cuts were never
     made; debt still up

LINK
1NC: Plan costs $50B (Expert 20)
2AC: 2. L/T: plan saves $
     by year 3
2NR: U controls direction: if
     U holds, savings barely
     matter
2AR: If N/U wins, L/T is
     offense: plan cuts the
     deficit
```

*How to read it:* the 2AC's non-unique uses a thumper, the road bill. Both last speeches fight over uniqueness, because it decides which way the link points. The judge decides uniqueness first. That answer tells the judge whether the link or the link turn counts more.

## Common mistakes

- **Neg: old uniqueness evidence.** Fix: update uniqueness cards often. Facts about the status quo change.
- **Neg: ignoring thumpers.** Fix: answer each one in the block. Show how it is different from the plan.
- **Aff: "non-unique" with no evidence.** Fix: show the harm is already here. A bare claim is easy to answer.
- **Aff: a link turn without uniqueness.** Fix: pair the link turn with non-unique, so the plan stops a harm that is coming.
- **Both: no "brink" argument.** Fix: say how close the world is to the edge, and how big a push the plan gives.
- **Judges: deciding the link first.** Fix: settle uniqueness first, because it sets which way the link points.
