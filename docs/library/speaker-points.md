# Speaker points

> **Plain version.** Speaker points are a score the judge gives each debater, apart from who wins.
>
> - **The scale:** Most leagues use a scale near 25 to 30, with tenths allowed, such as 28.4. Higher is better.
> - **What earns points:** Points reward clear speaking, smart choices, good cross-ex (question time) and respect for others.
> - **Why fairness matters:** Points help break ties in the standings (the team rankings) and decide speaker awards. So being fair and consistent matters.

## How the scale works

Every league sets its own scale. Always check the ballot or the tournament's instructions first. Some use whole numbers. Some use tenths or halves. Some use a different range. The guide below is for the common 30-point scale with tenths.

**Rough guide** (it varies by league and by level):

| Points | What it usually means |
|---|---|
| 29.5 – 30 | Outstanding. Among the best speeches you expect to see at this level. Rare. |
| 29.0 – 29.4 | Excellent. Clear, smart and in control of the round. |
| 28.5 – 28.9 | Very good. Strong choices, few errors. |
| 28.0 – 28.4 | Good. Solid and clear, with some missed chances. |
| 27.5 – 27.9 | Average for the level. Did the job, with real gaps. |
| 27.0 – 27.4 | Below average. Hard to follow, or dropped (did not answer) key arguments. |
| 26.0 – 26.9 | Weak round. Major problems with clarity or strategy. |
| Below 26 | Usually kept for serious problems, such as rude or offensive conduct. Many leagues ask the judge to explain. |

**Judge the level.** A strong novice and a strong varsity debater can both earn a 29 in their own division. Compare each debater to others at the same level.

**Point ranges drift.** In many circuits (regional groups of tournaments), the average has moved up over time. Ask the tournament or a coach what a typical point is if you are not sure.

## What earns points

- **Clarity.** Can the judge understand the words and follow the order? That includes the **tags**: the one-line claim a debater says before each quote of evidence (a card). Speed is fine only if it stays clear.
- **Organization.** A roadmap (the order of the speech, said at the start), signposts (words that say where the speaker is, such as "On the DA..."), and answers that match the flow (your notes).
- **Strategy.** Good choices: answering every argument, going for the right arguments in the rebuttals (the last four speeches), not wasting time.
- **Explanation.** Saying *why*, in the debater's own words, not just reading cards.
- **Cross-ex.** Sharp questions and honest, clear answers.
- **Respect.** Courtesy to opponents, partner and judge.

## Points and the win

- Points are **separate** from the decision. A debater on the losing team can earn the highest points in the round.
- A "low-point win" is when the winning team gets lower total points than the losing team. Many tournaments forbid or flag a low-point win. Follow your tournament's rule.
- Points should reflect **this round**, not a debater's reputation or record.

## Giving points fairly

1. **Decide the winner first.** Then give points.
2. **Use the same scale all day.** Write down the points you gave in each round, so you stay consistent.
3. **Do not punish style alone.** A debater who speaks in a way you do not prefer can still earn high points if they are clear and smart.
4. **Explain on the ballot.** One or two lines per debater: one thing done well, one thing to work on.
5. **Never use points to punish who a debater is.** Points rate the debating, not the person.

## Example in flow shorthand

A judge's margin notes for points, written during the round.

Key: "1A" is the first aff (affirmative) speaker, and "2N" is the second neg (negative) speaker. "ext" = extended. "IL on DA" = a middle step of a disadvantage (an argument that the plan causes harm). "off" = the neg's off-case arguments. "CX" = cross-ex. "T" = topicality (does the plan fit the topic?).

```
1A (gave the 1AC and 1AR)
  clear, ext case well in 1AR;
  dropped IL on DA
  Pts: 28.3

2A (gave the 2AC and 2AR)
  answered all off; strong 2AR
  weighing; 2AC slow
  Pts: 28.8

1N (gave the 1NC and 1NR)
  fast, unclear tags; good T
  in 1NR
  Pts: 27.9

2N (gave the 2NC and 2NR)
  great CX; 2NR well explained;
  2NC ran long on T
  Pts: 28.7
```

*How to read it:* the judge notes one strength and one weakness for each debater, then gives points. The 2A and 2N are close. The 1N lost points for unclear tags, even though their topicality work was good.

## Common mistakes

- **Giving the same points to everyone.** Fix: use the range to show real differences. Points are useful only if they differ.
- **Matching points to the win every time.** Fix: rate each speaker. The best speaker can be on the losing team.
- **Using a scale from a different league.** Fix: read the ballot's instructions and use the local scale.
- **Low points with no reason.** Fix: if you give a low score, explain why on the ballot.
- **Rating speed or style instead of clarity.** Fix: ask whether you could follow the arguments, not whether you liked the style.
