# The politics DA

> **Plain version.** The politics disadvantage says the plan has a political cost.
>
> - **How it works:** Leaders have only so much support to spend. If the leader must spend it on the plan, there is less left for another goal the leader cares about, and that goal fails.
> - **The harm:** The failure of that other goal.
> - **The aff often answers:** the plan is popular, the other goal will fail or pass anyway, or this kind of argument does not fit the debate's rules about imagining the plan passes.

## The generic shape

A **disadvantage** (DA) is a neg argument that the plan causes something bad. It has four parts. See [Disadvantages](disadvantages.md). Here is how the politics DA fills them in.

| Part | The politics DA says | Example (generic) |
|---|---|---|
| **Uniqueness** | The leader's priority will pass now | "The leader's top bill has enough votes today." |
| **Link** | The plan costs the leader support | "Lawmakers dislike the plan, so pushing it costs the leader goodwill." |
| **Internal link** | Support is limited, and the priority needs it | "Without that goodwill, the leader cannot keep the votes for the top bill." |
| **Impact** | The priority's failure is bad | "The top bill fails, and the economy suffers." |

**Political capital** is the key idea. It means the goodwill, trust and favors a leader can use to get lawmakers to vote a certain way. The DA treats it like a budget: spend it on one thing, and less is left for another.

## Common versions

- **Capital version:** the plan is a fight, and the fight uses up capital the priority needs. This is the classic shape.
- **Popularity version:** the plan is unpopular with voters or lawmakers, so the leader loses standing.
- **Elections version:** the plan changes how voters feel, so the result of a coming election changes, and that result leads to the impact.

Each version needs current evidence. Politics changes week to week, so a politics DA is only as good as its newest uniqueness card.

## The usual answers

These answers are for the aff, usually in the 2AC (the second affirmative constructive).

- **Non-unique:** the priority will fail anyway. It lacks votes for other reasons.
- **Thumper:** the leader is already in other political fights. If those fights did not sink the priority, the plan will not.
- **No link: the plan is popular** with lawmakers or voters, or it would pass quietly with little fight.
- **Link turn: winners win.** Some evidence says a leader who wins a hard fight gains capital, because success builds strength. If so, the plan helps the priority.
- **No internal link: capital is not key.** Lawmakers vote on the priority for their own reasons, such as local interests. The leader's capital does not decide it.
- **No impact:** the priority would not do what the neg says, or its failure is not that harmful.
- **Case outweighs:** the plan's benefit is bigger than the harm of the priority failing.

As with any DA, do not link turn and impact turn the same politics DA. "The plan helps the priority pass" plus "the priority is bad" argues the plan does harm. See [Links and link turns](links.md).

## The fiat debate

**Fiat** is the shared idea that the aff gets to imagine the plan passes. It keeps the round on whether the plan *should* happen, not whether it *would*. See [Fiat](fiat.md).

The politics DA raises a hard question about fiat, because its link often comes from the *fight to pass* the plan. The aff often says fiat means the plan passes at once, with no fight, and that it is unfair to make the aff debate the leader's whole agenda. The neg often says fiat decides that the plan passes, not how, and that real policymakers weigh political cost. Many judges accept politics DAs, and some give them little weight on fiat grounds. See [Fiat](fiat.md) for the full arguments on both sides.

## How judges weigh it

- **Uniqueness is time-sensitive.** Judges often prefer the newest evidence on whether the priority will pass, if a team points out the dates.
- **Link specificity matters.** Evidence that lawmakers dislike this kind of plan beats evidence that the leader is "busy."
- **The internal link is often the weak point.** Does capital really decide the priority? Many judges ask this.
- **Clear explanation helps with lay judges.** A judge with no debate background can follow "the leader has limited support" if it is told as a simple story.

## Example in flow shorthand

A generic politics DA. The middle speeches are left out to save space. Each block is one part of the DA, with each speech's line on it, top to bottom. Key: "N/U" means non-unique, "L/T" means link turn, "ev" means evidence, "Ext" means extend, and "->" means "leads to". Names in parentheses are invented cards.

```
DA: POLITICS

UNIQUENESS
1NC: Leader's priority bill
     passes now (Analyst 23).
2AC: 1. N/U: bill lacks votes
     (Author 23).
2NR: Newer ev: deal is set
     (Analyst, this week).
2AR: Their ev is one lawmaker's
     view.

LINK
1NC: Plan unpopular -> costs
     capital.
2AC: 2. L/T: winners win; a hard
     win builds capital.
2NR: Winners win fails for
     unpopular plans.
2AR: Ext L/T: a win shows
     strength.

THUMPER
2AC: 3. Thumper: leader is
     already in a budget fight.
2NR: Budget fight is over;
     capital is back.
2AR: (no answer)

FIAT
2AC: 4. Fiat: plan passes at
     once; no fight to cost
     capital.
2NR: Fiat says plan passes, not
     how; cost is a real effect.
2AR: Unfair to make aff debate
     the whole agenda.

IMPACT
1NC: Priority fails -> economic
     harm.
2AC: 5. No impact.
2NR: Ext 1NC impact.
2AR: (no answer)
```

*How to read it:* the 2AR does not answer the 2NR on the thumper, so the thumper is gone. It also gives no answer on the impact row. The judge must decide the fiat question, and then whether winners win beats the capital link.

## Common mistakes

- **Neg: old uniqueness.** Fix: update the priority's status before every tournament.
- **Neg: a link that says only "the leader is busy."** Fix: show that lawmakers dislike this kind of plan, or that the fight would be costly.
- **Aff: ignoring the internal link.** Fix: ask why capital decides the priority. It is often the weakest step.
- **Aff: winners win plus "the priority is bad."** Fix: that is a double turn. Pick one.
- **Both: a fiat argument with no reason.** Fix: explain why the link is or is not fair under fiat, not just that it is.
- **Judges: deciding the fiat question before the round.** Fix: judge the theory as argued, unless your paradigm says otherwise and the teams knew it.
