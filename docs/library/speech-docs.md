# Speech docs and email chains

> **Plain version.** Most policy debaters type their evidence into a file, called a speech document or speech doc, before they read it.
>
> - **How it is shared:** Before each speech, the team sends it to the other team and the judge. They often use an email thread called the email chain, or a shared folder.
> - **What it is for:** The doc lets everyone read the cards as they are spoken.
> - **The doc is not the speech:** The judge decides from what was said out loud, as written on the flow (the judge's notes).

## What a speech doc is

A **speech doc** is a file with the cards a debater plans to read in one speech. A **card** is one piece of evidence: a tag (the debater's one-line claim), a cite (who wrote it and where) and the quoted text. See [Evidence and cards](evidence-and-cards.md).

The doc is in the order the debater plans to read. It shows which words will be read out loud, usually by highlighting them. The rest of the text stays in the doc for context.

## What is shared

Teams usually share:

- **Every card** the speaker plans to read, in order.
- **Headings** that name each sheet (each argument's own section of the document), such as "DA: Spending" or "Case."
- **The plan text or counterplan text,** word for word, when there is one.

Teams differ on **analytics**. An analytic is an argument made without a card, using reasoning alone. Some teams put their analytics in the doc. Many do not. Unless a league rule says otherwise, a team usually does not have to share analytics. Some judges ask for them in their paradigm (a short note on how they judge).

The doc goes to the other team and to the judge, at the same time. Many teams send it right before the speech starts. How this counts toward prep time varies by judge. See [Prep time](prep-time.md).

## How the sharing works

There are two common ways:

- **An email chain.** One debater starts an email thread with both teams and the judge. Each speaker replies to the thread with their doc before they speak.
- **A shared folder or file space.** Teams upload each doc to a folder everyone can open. Some tournaments provide their own system.

Some rounds use a flash drive or a printed copy instead. Some leagues, or some lay divisions (divisions judged mostly by people new to debate), do not share docs at all. Follow the tournament's rules.

## The judge on the chain

As a judge, you can ask to be on the chain. Many judges do. Debaters often ask at the start: "What email should we use?"

**Why join:**

- You can read along when a speaker is fast or hard to hear.
- You can check a card that matters to the decision.
- You can see the exact wording of the plan or a counterplan.

**Things to know:**

- **You do not have to read every word.** Listen first. Look at the doc when you need to.
- **Use an email you are happy to share** with students and coaches. Some judges use a separate address just for debate.
- **Do not forward the docs** outside the round, unless the tournament or the teams say you may.
- **Do not judge from the doc.** See the next sections.

## Marking cards

Sometimes a debater stops reading a card before the end of the highlighted part. Maybe time ran out. Maybe they chose to skip ahead. When this happens, the debater should say so out loud:

- "Mark the card at 'lower emergency costs.'"
- "Cut the card there."
- "Skip the next card."

To **mark** a card means to show where the reading stopped. After the speech, the other team can ask for a **marked copy**: the doc with the marks added, and with skipped cards noted. Many judges expect teams to send one when asked.

Marking matters for fairness. If a debater skips words but the doc shows them as read, the judge and the other team will think more evidence was read than really was. Skipping highlighted words without marking them is called **clipping**. Most leagues treat it as a serious ethics violation. See [Round conduct, safety and evidence ethics](round-conduct.md).

## Why the doc does not replace the flow

The doc and the speech are not the same thing.

- **The doc shows cards, not explanation.** Rebuttals are mostly explanation, comparison and analytics. Much of that is never in the doc.
- **The doc shows what was planned, not what was read.** A speaker may skip a card, mark a card, or add an argument on the fly.
- **Answers line up on the flow, not in the doc.** The flow shows which answer goes with which argument, and which arguments were dropped (never answered). A doc cannot show a drop.

So most judges follow one rule: **a card counts only as far as it was read out loud.** If it is in the doc but the speaker skipped it, it was not part of the speech. Judges differ on a few edge cases, such as a team asking the judge to "insert" a re-highlighted version of a card without reading it. Many judges say in their paradigm how they handle that.

Keep flowing, even if you have the doc. See [How to flow](how-to-flow.md).

## Example in flow shorthand

A 1NC doc next to the speech as given, and the judge's flow. A **DA** (disadvantage) says the plan causes something bad. Its parts here are U (uniqueness: the bad thing is not happening now), L (link: the plan causes it) and IL (internal link: the step from the link to the harm). "One off" means one argument besides the answers to the aff case. "$" means money.

```
DOC: heading "DA: Spending"
SAID: "One off, the spending DA"
FLOW: DA: Spending

DOC: U card (Analyst 21)
SAID: read in full
FLOW: U: budget tight
  (Analyst 21)

DOC: L card (Expert 20)
SAID: "mark it at 'per year'"
FLOW: L: plan costs $
  (Expert 20) - marked

DOC: IL card (Writer 19)
SAID: "skip this card"
FLOW: (not read: not flowed)

DOC: (no IL analytic)
SAID: "cost forces cuts: the
  law says so" (analytic)
FLOW: IL: law forces cuts
  when $ is short (analytic)
```

*How to read it:* the flow shows what the speech really did (a marked card, a skipped card, a new analytic); the doc alone would not.

## Common mistakes

- **Debaters: not marking a card you cut short.** Fix: say "mark the card at..." out loud, and send a marked copy if asked.
- **Debaters: sending the doc late or to the wrong people.** Fix: set up the chain before the round, with both teams and the judge.
- **Debaters: assuming the judge reads the doc.** Fix: speak clearly and explain each card. The judge decides from the speech.
- **Judges: reading the doc instead of flowing.** Fix: flow the speech. Use the doc to check a word or a card.
- **Judges: counting cards that were not read.** Fix: weigh only what was read out loud in the round.
- **Everyone: sharing docs outside the round without permission.** Fix: follow the tournament's and the teams' wishes.
