# The economy DA

> **Plain version.** The economy disadvantage says the plan hurts the economy. The plan might raise costs for businesses, add new rules, or make people unsure about the future. That slows growth. The negative then argues that a weaker economy leads to real harm, such as lost jobs, poverty, or even conflict between countries. The affirmative usually answers that the plan is too small to matter, that it helps the economy, or that the economy is strong enough to recover.

## The generic shape

A **disadvantage** (DA) is a neg argument that the plan causes something bad. It has four parts. See [Disadvantages](disadvantages.md). Here is how the economy DA fills them in.

| Part | The economy DA says | Example (generic) |
|---|---|---|
| **Uniqueness** | The economy is growing or stable now | "Growth is steady this year." |
| **Link** | The plan slows growth | "The plan raises costs for businesses, so they hire less." |
| **Internal link** | Slower growth leads to a downturn, and a downturn leads to harm | "A small slowdown can tip a fragile economy into decline." |
| **Impact** | The harm is bad | "Economic decline makes conflict more likely." |

**Growth** here means the economy producing more over time. More growth usually means more jobs and more money for public goods. The DA treats growth as fragile: a push in the wrong direction can tip it.

## Common link stories

The link is how the plan touches the economy. Common generic versions:

- **Cost link.** The plan adds costs for businesses or people. They spend and hire less.
- **Rules link.** The plan adds new rules. Firms must spend time and money to follow them.
- **Uncertainty link.** The plan makes businesses unsure about future policy. Unsure businesses wait to invest.
- **Spending link.** The plan adds to public debt, and debt slows growth later. This overlaps with [The spending DA](example-spending-da.md).

A link that names what this plan does to a real part of the economy is stronger than a link that says "new policies hurt business."

## Common impact stories

The impact is why a weaker economy matters. Common generic versions:

- **Conflict.** In hard times, leaders may start fights abroad to unite people at home. Or countries compete harder for scarce resources.
- **Human harm.** A downturn means lost jobs, poverty and worse health.
- **Lost capacity.** A weaker economy has less money for defense, aid or other goals.

Each impact story adds steps to the chain. Each step is a place the aff can attack. See [Internal links](internal-links.md).

## The usual answers

These answers are for the aff, usually in the 2AC (the second affirmative constructive).

- **Non-unique: the economy is weak anyway.** If a downturn is already coming, the plan does not cause it.
- **Thumper.** Other recent policies or events should have hurt growth already. If they did not, the plan will not.
- **No link: the plan is too small.** Its cost is tiny next to the whole economy.
- **No link: the economy is too strong for this plan to move it.** If growth is very strong, one small push will not tip it. Note that strong growth agrees with the neg's uniqueness. So this is not a non-unique answer. It is an argument about link size. See [Uniqueness](uniqueness.md).
- **Link turn: the plan helps growth.** It creates jobs, builds something useful, or prevents a costly harm. If so, the DA becomes a reason to vote aff.
- **No internal link: resilience.** Economies absorb shocks and recover. One policy does not cause a downturn.
- **Impact defense: decline does not cause conflict.** The aff reads evidence that hard times do not make wars more likely.
- **Case outweighs.** The plan's benefit is bigger, more likely, or comes sooner.

### The impact turn: growth bad

Some teams answer with an **impact turn**: an argument that the "bad" result is actually good. Here, that means arguing that slower growth would be good. Common generic reasons include harm to the environment, or the claim that a growth-focused economy causes other harms. Some of these arguments are made in policy style, and some in kritikal style.

Judges differ on this answer. Some judges are happy to weigh growth-bad arguments like any other impact turn. Others find them hard to win against well-prepared growth-good evidence. Like any impact turn, it works only if the aff wins that the result is truly good. And it must not be paired with a link turn on the same DA. That would be a **double turn**: "the plan helps growth" plus "growth is bad" argues the plan causes harm. See [Links and link turns](links.md) and [Impacts](impacts.md).

## How the neg extends it

In the neg block (the 2NC and 1NR), the neg usually:

1. **Defends uniqueness.** Shows the economy is stable now but could be tipped.
2. **Proves the link is real and big enough.** Explains why this plan reaches a part of the economy that matters.
3. **Answers resilience.** Explains why this shock is different, or why the economy is fragile now.
4. **Defends the impact.** Answers impact defense and any growth-bad turn.
5. **Weighs.** Often claims the DA's impact is very large, and that the plan turns the case if the advantage needs a strong economy. See [Turns case](turns-case.md).

## How judges weigh it

- **The chain is long.** Plan to slowdown, slowdown to downturn, downturn to conflict. Many judges ask how likely each step is, and how likely the whole chain is.
- **Uniqueness and link work together.** If the aff says the economy is strong, the neg needs a big link. If the economy is fragile, a smaller link may be enough.
- **Magnitude versus probability.** The DA often claims a huge impact with a low chance. The aff often claims a smaller, surer benefit. How to compare them is up to the teams. See [Impact calculus](impact-calculus.md).
- **Impact turns need full development.** A growth-bad turn read in one line and extended in one line is hard to vote on. Most judges want it explained and weighed.

## Example in flow shorthand

A generic economy DA. The middle speeches are left out to save space.

```
1NC                     2AC                     2NR                     2AR
DA: Economy
U: growth steady now    1. N/U: slowdown        Slowdown ev predates    Still weak; new
   (Analyst 22)            coming (Author 21)      new jobs data           data is one month
L: plan raises costs    2. L/T: plan creates    Jobs are short-term;    Ext L/T: jobs last
   -> firms hire less      jobs in building        costs are lasting       past the build
IL: small shock tips    3. No IL: economy is    Fragile now: low        (no answer)
   a fragile economy       resilient               reserves
I: decline -> conflict  4. No impact: hard      Ext 1NC impact          Ext no impact: hard
                           times don't cause                               times don't cause
                           war (Expert 20)                                 war
```

*How to read it:* on uniqueness, the 2AC says a slowdown is coming anyway. The 2NR says that evidence is older than new jobs data. The 2AR says the economy is still weak, and the new data is only one month. On the link, the 2AC reads a link turn: the plan creates building jobs. The 2NR says those jobs are short-term, but the costs last. The 2AR extends the link turn and says the jobs last past the building phase. On the internal link, the 2AC says the economy is resilient. The 2NR says it is fragile now, because reserves are low, and the 2AR does not answer that. On the impact, the 2AC reads impact defense. The 2NR only extends the 1NC impact. The 2AR extends the impact defense. The judge must decide the link turn first, and then how much the impact defense lowers the risk of conflict.

## Common mistakes

- **Neg: a link that says only "regulation hurts business."** Fix: explain what this plan does to a real part of the economy.
- **Neg: skipping the steps from slowdown to conflict.** Fix: say each step out loud, with evidence for each.
- **Aff: link turn plus growth bad.** Fix: that is a double turn. Pick one.
- **Aff: a growth-bad turn with no weighing.** Fix: explain why slower growth is good, and why that outweighs the neg's harms.
- **Both: no dates on economic evidence.** Fix: say when your evidence was written. The economy changes fast.
- **Judges: assuming any slowdown means war.** Fix: check each step as argued, and weigh the risk of the whole chain.
