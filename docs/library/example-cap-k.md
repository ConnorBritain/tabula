# The capitalism kritik (cap K)

> **Plain version.** The capitalism kritik, or cap K, says the plan relies on market thinking: the idea that profit and growth are the way to fix problems. It can apply to many plans, so teams run it often. The negative argues that this thinking causes the very harms the plan tries to fix, such as inequality. It offers an alternative: a different way to organize economic life, or a rejection of market thinking. The affirmative often answers that the plan still helps people now, that the alternative cannot work, or that markets do more good than harm.

## The setup

A **kritik** (K) questions the ideas behind the aff, not only the plan's results. See [Kritiks](kritiks.md). The capitalism kritik questions the market thinking behind a plan.

In this example, the aff's plan is a new program that gives tax credits to firms that make clean energy. The aff says the plan cuts pollution and creates jobs.

## The generic shape

| Part | The cap K says | Example (generic) |
|---|---|---|
| **Link** | The plan relies on market logic | "The plan uses profit to fix pollution. It asks the market to solve a harm the market made." |
| **Impact** | Market logic causes serious harm | "An economy built on profit drives inequality, overwork and damage to the environment." |
| **Alternative** | A different approach | "Reject the aff's market logic, and support groups that organize economic life around need, not profit." |
| **Framework** | How the judge should decide | "Weigh the aff's economic assumptions first, because they shape what the plan can do." |

### The link

Cap K links usually point to one of these:

- **Market tools.** The plan uses tax credits, prices or incentives to change behavior.
- **Growth.** The aff's advantage says the plan grows the economy, and treats growth as good.
- **Reform.** The plan fixes one harm while leaving the larger system in place. The neg may say this makes the system more stable and harder to change.

A specific link is stronger than "every plan supports capitalism." See [The kritik link](kritik-links.md).

### The impact

Advocates of the cap K argue that a profit-driven economy is not neutral. They say it puts profit ahead of human need, concentrates wealth, and treats the environment and workers as costs to cut. Many versions also say the system produces repeated crises, and that reforms only delay the next one.

The neg often argues that the **K turns the case**: market logic will undercut the plan's own advantages. In this example, the neg might say the credits will go to large firms that do little to cut pollution. See [Turns case](turns-case.md).

### The alternative

The alternative varies. Some alts ask the judge to reject the aff's market logic. Some ask the judge to endorse a way of analyzing the economy that focuses on who owns and controls production. Some point to groups and movements that organize work and resources outside the market. See [The alternative](kritik-alternative.md).

## The usual aff answers

The aff usually answers in the 2AC, the second affirmative constructive. A full answer picks several of these.

- **Framework.** The judge should weigh the plan's results against the present system or another policy. See [Framework against a kritik](framework-vs-kritik.md).
- **Perm.** The aff can do the plan and the alternative together. For example, pass the credits and also support the groups the alt names. See [Permutations](permutations.md).
- **No link.** The plan is a public policy, not a market. Or the link is too general to be a reason to reject this aff.
- **Alt fails.** The alt has no clear path to replace markets, or no actor who would do it. Ask in cross-ex what the alt does.
- **Case outweighs.** The plan cuts pollution now. That benefit is real and near, while the K's harms are slow or uncertain.
- **Impact turn: capitalism is good.** An impact turn argues that what the neg calls bad is good. Some affs argue that market economies have reduced poverty and driven new technology, and that efforts to replace them carry serious risks. This defends an economic system. It does not defend the harms the K names. It is a real position in economics and politics, and it is also a common neg target.

Two warnings. First, **an impact turn and a perm do not mix well.** If the aff says capitalism is good, it is hard to also say it will do an alt that rejects capitalism. The neg will point this out. See [Answering a kritik](answering-kritiks.md). Second, **framework alone is rarely enough.** If the aff loses framework and has nothing else, it loses the round.

## The usual neg extensions

- **Links are disadvantages to the perm.** The perm still uses market logic, so it still links.
- **K turns case.** The plan's benefits will not happen, because the market will steer them elsewhere.
- **Alt solves the root cause.** The alt addresses why the harm exists, while the plan treats only one symptom.
- **Answers to cap good.** Growth's gains go mostly to a few, and its harms to people and the environment outweigh them.

## Example in flow shorthand

A generic cap K against the clean energy tax credit plan. The middle speeches are left out. Each block is one part of the K, with each speech's line on it, top to bottom. Key: "squo" means status quo (the present system), "->" means "leads to", "=" means "is", and "Ext" means extend. Names in parentheses are invented cards.

```
FRAMEWORK (FW)
1NC: Weigh the aff's market logic
     first.
2AC: 1. Weigh plan vs squo or a
     policy.
2NR: Ideas first; plan is built
     on profit.
2AR: Weigh plan. Even if ideas
     count, cap good.

LINK
1NC: Credits use profit to fix
     harm (Theorist 16).
2AC: 2. No link: credits are
     public policy.
2NR: Credits pay firms for
     profit; that is the link.
2AR: (not extended)

IMPACT
1NC: Profit drive -> inequality,
     harm to environment
     (Scholar 21).
2AC: 3. Cap good: growth cuts
     poverty (Econ 19).
2NR: Growth gains go to a few;
     harms outweigh.
2AR: Ext cap good: growth cut
     poverty (Econ 19).

ALTERNATIVE (ALT)
1NC: Reject market logic; build
     groups outside the market.
2AC: 4. Perm: plan + alt.
2NR: Perm contradicts "cap good."
2AR: (not extended)

ALT FAILS
2AC: 5. Alt fails: no path to
     replace markets.
2NR: Alt = groups already
     forming; it grows.
2AR: No card that groups can
     replace markets.

CASE OUTWEIGHS
2AC: 6. Case outweighs.
2NR: K turns case: credits go to
     big firms.
2AR: Credits cut emissions now;
     turn is slow.
```

*How to read it:* the 2AC makes six answers, including both "cap good" and a perm, and the 2NR says those two contradict. The 2AR drops the perm, so the contradiction no longer matters. The 2AR also drops "no link," so the neg's link stands. It extends four answers: framework (with an "even if"), cap good, alt fails and case weighing. The round likely turns on two questions: who wins the cap good debate, and how much of the case survives the K turns case argument?

## How judges tend to weigh it

- **Framework first.** Most judges decide framework before anything else. Under "weigh the plan," the cap K must show the plan is a worse policy. Under "ideas first," the link may be enough.
- **Link specificity.** Many judges reward a link to this plan's mechanism over a link to "all policies."
- **The alt.** Some judges are skeptical of an alt that cannot say who acts or how. Others accept an alt that is mainly a way of analyzing the problem. Teams should argue which applies.
- **Cap good.** Judges differ on impact turns of this kind. Some find them a strong, honest answer. Others think they are hard to win against a well-evidenced K. Both views are common. Decide on the evidence and explanation in the round.
- **Lay judges.** A judge with little debate background will follow a plain story best: what the plan assumes, why that is harmful, and what the alt does instead. The aff should explain just as plainly why the plan still helps.

## Common mistakes

- **Neg: a link to "all policies."** Fix: point to this plan's tool, such as its tax credits, and explain why that tool links.
- **Neg: an alt no one can explain.** Fix: say in two sentences what the alt does and who does it.
- **Aff: cap good plus a perm.** Fix: choose one story by the 1AR, or explain why they do not conflict.
- **Aff: forgetting the case.** Fix: in every speech, say why cutting pollution now outweighs the K's harms.
- **Both: treating cap good as a joke or as obvious.** Fix: debate it with evidence, like any other impact.
- **Judges: deciding on your own view of the economy.** Fix: decide on what the teams proved, and explain it in the RFD.
