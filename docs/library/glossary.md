# Policy debate glossary

> **Plain version.** Debate has a lot of special words. This page lists them in A to Z order, with one plain sentence each. When a term has its own page, the term links to it. If you are new, start with [Debate 101](debate-101.md), and come back here when you hear a word you do not know.

## Numbers

- **1AC** (first affirmative constructive): the first speech; the aff reads its plan and case. See [Speeches and times](speeches-and-times.md).
- **1AR** (first affirmative rebuttal): the aff's 5-minute answer to the whole negative block.
- **1NC** (first negative constructive): the neg's first speech, where it starts all its arguments.
- **1NR** (first negative rebuttal): the second half of the negative block.
- **2AC** (second affirmative constructive): the aff's answers to everything in the 1NC.
- **2AR** (second affirmative rebuttal): the last speech of the round.
- **2NC** (second negative constructive): the first half of the negative block.
- **2NR** (second negative rebuttal): the neg's last speech, where it picks what to win on.

## A

- **Add-on:** a new advantage the aff reads in the 2AC. Many judges allow it because the 2AC is a constructive speech. See [Advantages](advantages.md).
- **Advantage:** a reason the plan is good; part of the aff case. See [Advantages](advantages.md).
- **Affirmative (aff):** the team that supports the resolution and defends a plan. See [The affirmative case](affirmative-case.md).
- **Agent counterplan:** a counterplan where a different actor does the plan. See [Common counterplans](common-counterplans.md).
- **Alternate cause:** another cause of the harm that the plan does not fix, so the plan solves less. See [Solvency](solvency.md).
- **Alternative (alt):** what a kritik offers instead of the aff. See [The alternative](kritik-alternative.md).
- **Analytic:** an argument made without a card, using reasoning alone.

## B

- **Ballot:** the form where the judge records the winner, points and reason. See [The ballot and the RFD](ballot-and-rfd.md).
- **Block:** the 2NC and 1NR together, 13 minutes of neg speaking in a row.
- **Bright line:** a clear test for where a topicality or theory interpretation draws its limit, so both teams know what counts. See [Interpretation and violation](topicality-interpretation.md).
- **Brink:** the point where a small push causes the impact; a DA says the status quo is near it and the plan pushes past it. See [Uniqueness](uniqueness.md).
- **Burden of proof:** the duty to prove a claim; the aff carries it for its case.

## C

- **Card:** one piece of evidence: a tag, a cite and a quoted text. See [Evidence and cards](evidence-and-cards.md).
- **Case:** the aff's plan and the arguments for it.
- **Case turn:** a neg argument that turns the aff's case into a reason to vote neg: the plan makes its own problem worse, or the aff's "good" result is actually bad. See [Case turns and case defense](case-turns.md).
- **Circumvention:** the claim that people will stall, dodge or undo the plan after it passes; a solvency argument. See [Solvency](solvency.md).
- **Cite:** who wrote a card, when, and where it was published.
- **Clash:** direct disagreement between the teams on the same point.
- **Clipping:** claiming to read more of a card than was read out loud; a serious ethics violation. See [Round conduct](round-conduct.md).
- **Competition:** the test of whether a counterplan is a real alternative to the plan. See [Competition](competition.md).
- **Concession:** something a team admits, or does not answer. A dropped argument is often treated as conceded.
- **Conditionality (condo):** the neg's choice to drop a counterplan or kritik later in the round. See [Conditionality](conditionality.md).
- **Constructive:** one of the first four speeches, where teams build arguments.
- **Consult counterplan:** a counterplan that asks another party first, and does the plan only if they agree. See [Example: consult CP](example-consult-cp.md).
- **Counter-interpretation (CI):** the responding team's own interpretation, in a topicality or theory debate. See [Topicality](topicality.md).
- **Counterplan (CP):** a neg policy that is offered instead of the plan. See [Counterplans](counterplans.md).
- **Counterplan text:** the exact wording of what the counterplan does. See [Counterplan text](counterplan-text.md).
- **Critic of argument:** a judge who weighs the quality of ideas, not only policy results. See [Judge paradigms](judge-paradigms.md).
- **Cross-examination (CX):** 3 minutes of questions after each constructive. See [Cross-examination](cross-examination.md).

## D

- **Defense:** an answer that makes an argument smaller or stops it (for example, no link), but does not give a reason to vote for you. See [Answering a DA](answering-disadvantages.md).
- **Disadvantage (DA):** a neg argument that the plan causes something bad. See [Disadvantages](disadvantages.md).
- **Disclosure:** telling the debaters the decision after the round. Some tournaments allow it and some do not. See [Giving feedback](giving-feedback.md).
- **Dispositional (dispo):** a counterplan the neg can drop only under set conditions, often only if the aff makes a perm. See [Conditionality](conditionality.md).
- **Double turn:** making both a link turn and an impact turn on one DA, which helps the other team.
- **Drop:** an argument that got no answer. Most judges treat it as true, if it came with a reason.

## E

- **Education:** a common standard or voter: which choice helps debaters learn the most.
- **Effects topicality:** the argument that the plan becomes topical only through its effects, not by what it does directly. See [Effects and extra topicality](effects-and-extra-topicality.md).
- **Email chain:** the shared email or folder where teams send speech documents. See [Speech docs](speech-docs.md).
- **Even if:** a weighing phrase that says you win even if the other team is right on a point.
- **Evidence:** quoted expert material read in a speech. See [Evidence and cards](evidence-and-cards.md).
- **Existential inherency:** the claim that the problem exists, so something must be stopping a fix; some judges accept it and others think it is weak. See [Inherency](inherency.md).
- **Extend (ext):** carry an argument forward into a later speech, with its reason.
- **Extra topicality:** a part of the plan that goes beyond the resolution. See [Effects and extra topicality](effects-and-extra-topicality.md).

## F

- **Fairness:** a common standard or voter: whether both teams had an equal chance.
- **Fiat:** the idea that we imagine the plan is passed, so the debate is about whether it should be, not whether it would be. See [Fiat](fiat.md).
- **Floating PIK:** a kritik alternative that, the aff says, could include the plan, so the neg gets the aff's benefits too; teams debate whether it is fair. See [The alternative](kritik-alternative.md).
- **Flow:** the notes of the round, in columns, one per speech. See [How to flow](how-to-flow.md).
- **Framework (FW):** the debate about how the judge should decide the round. See [Framework](framework.md).

## G

- **Games player:** a judge who sees debate as a game with fair rules. See [Judge paradigms](judge-paradigms.md).
- **Ground:** the arguments a team can fairly make. A standard in topicality and theory.

## H

- **Harms:** the problem in the status quo that the aff wants to fix; a stock issue. See [Harms](harms.md).
- **Hypothesis tester:** a judge who treats the resolution like a claim to be tested. See [Judge paradigms](judge-paradigms.md).

## I

- **Impact:** the result at the end of an argument, and why it matters. See [Impacts](impacts.md).
- **Impact calculus:** comparing impacts by size, chance, timing and more. See [Impact calculus](impact-calculus.md).
- **Impact turn:** an argument that the other team's "bad" result is actually good.
- **Inherency:** why the problem is not being solved now; a stock issue. See [Inherency](inherency.md).
- **Internal link (IL):** the step between a link and an impact. See [Internal links](internal-links.md).
- **International fiat:** imagining that another country or a world body acts. Teams debate whether the neg may do this. See [Common theory arguments](common-theory.md).
- **Interpretation:** a team's meaning of a word in the resolution. See [Interpretation and violation](topicality-interpretation.md).
- **Intrinsic perm:** a perm that adds an action that is in neither the plan nor the counterplan; many judges see it as unfair. See [Permutations](permutations.md).

## K

- **K v K:** a round where a kritikal aff meets a kritik from the neg; teams debate whether perms apply in a debate about methods. See [Kritikal affirmatives](kritikal-affirmatives.md).
- **Kritik (K):** an argument that questions the ideas or assumptions behind the aff. See [Kritiks](kritiks.md).
- **Kritikal affirmative (K aff):** an aff that does not defend a usual plan, or that criticizes ideas. See [Kritikal affirmatives](kritikal-affirmatives.md).

## L

- **Lay judge:** a judge with little or no debate background, often a parent. See [Judging your first round](first-round-judge.md).
- **Limits:** a topicality standard about keeping the number of possible affs manageable. See [Standards](topicality-standards.md).
- **Link (L):** how the plan starts the chain toward an impact. See [Links](links.md).
- **Link turn (L/T):** an argument that the plan does the opposite of what the link says.

## M

- **Magnitude:** how big an impact is.
- **Marked copy:** the speech doc with marks that show where a debater stopped reading each card. See [Speech docs](speech-docs.md).
- **Multiple actor fiat:** imagining that several separate actors all act at once, often in a counterplan. Its fairness is debated. See [Common theory arguments](common-theory.md).
- **Mutual exclusivity:** the plan and counterplan cannot both happen. One test of competition.

## N

- **Negative (neg):** the team that opposes the resolution or the plan.
- **Net benefit:** a reason the counterplan alone is better than doing both; often a DA. See [Net benefits](net-benefits.md).
- **New in the 2:** a new argument made in the last rebuttals; most judges do not count it.
- **No link:** an answer that the plan does not cause what an argument says. See [Links and link turns](links.md).
- **Non-unique (N/U):** the bad thing will happen anyway, so the plan does not cause it.

## O

- **Object fiat:** the neg imagining that the target of the plan (for example, another country) acts; teams debate whether it is fair. See [Common theory arguments](common-theory.md).
- **Off-case:** neg arguments that start on their own sheets (T, DA, CP, K).
- **Offense:** an argument that gives the judge a reason to vote for your side, such as a turn or your own advantage. See [Answering a DA](answering-disadvantages.md).
- **On-case:** neg arguments that answer the aff case directly.
- **Oral critique:** spoken feedback from the judge after the round. See [Giving feedback](giving-feedback.md).
- **Overview:** a short summary at the top of a sheet that frames the argument.

## P

- **Paradigm:** a judge's way of deciding rounds, often posted online. See [Judge paradigms](judge-paradigms.md).
- **Perm do both:** the most common permutation: do the whole plan and the whole counterplan together. See [Permutations](permutations.md).
- **Permutation (perm):** the aff's test that it can do the plan and the counterplan, or the alt, together. See [Permutations](permutations.md).
- **PIC** (plan-inclusive counterplan): a counterplan that does most of the plan but leaves out one part. See [Example: PICs](example-pic.md).
- **PIK** (plan-inclusive kritik): a kritik whose alternative does the plan but rejects one word, idea or part of how the aff justified it. Teams debate whether it is fair. See [PICs and word PICs](example-pic.md).
- **Plan:** the aff's one-sentence statement of what it will do.
- **Policymaker:** a judge who weighs costs and benefits like a lawmaker. See [Judge paradigms](judge-paradigms.md).
- **Politics DA:** a DA saying the plan uses up a leader's support, so another policy fails. See [Example: politics DA](example-politics-da.md).
- **Precision:** a topicality standard: the meaning that experts in the field use.
- **Predictability:** a topicality standard: a meaning both teams could expect before the round.
- **Prep time:** time each team can use to plan its speeches. See [Prep time](prep-time.md).
- **Presumption:** which team wins if nothing else decides the round. See [Presumption](presumption.md).
- **Probability:** how likely an impact is.
- **Process counterplan:** a counterplan that does the plan through a different process (for example, after a review or a vote). Its competition is often debated. See [Common counterplan shapes](common-counterplans.md).

## R

- **Reasonability:** a judge standard where the aff wins topicality if it is reasonably topical. See [Voters](topicality-voters.md).
- **Rebuttal:** one of the last four speeches, used to extend and weigh.
- **Reject the argument / reject the team:** two possible results of winning a theory argument. See [Reasons to reject](reject-the-team.md).
- **Resolution:** the season's topic sentence.
- **Reverse voting issue (RVI):** a claim that a team should win because the other side's theory or topicality argument was wrong. It is usually made by the aff. Many judges do not vote on RVIs. See [Theory](theory.md).
- **Reversibility:** whether an impact can be undone.
- **RFD** (reason for decision): the judge's explanation of the result. See [The ballot and the RFD](ballot-and-rfd.md).
- **Roadmap:** the order of sheets a speaker will cover, said before the speech.
- **Role of the ballot (ROB):** a claim about what the judge's vote stands for. See [Role of the ballot](role-of-the-ballot.md).

## S

- **Scope:** how widely an impact spreads.
- **Severance perm:** a perm that drops part of the plan; many judges see it as unfair. See [Permutations](permutations.md).
- **Sheet:** one page or tab of the flow, for one big argument.
- **Significance:** how big the harms or the solvency are; a stock issue. See [Significance](significance.md).
- **Signpost:** a word that tells the judge where you are ("On the DA...").
- **Solvency:** whether the plan solves the problem; a stock issue. See [Solvency](solvency.md).
- **Solvency advocate:** a card that says the exact plan or counterplan would work. Some judges expect one. See [Solvency deficits](solvency-deficits.md).
- **Solvency deficit:** a part of the case the counterplan cannot solve. See [Solvency deficits](solvency-deficits.md).
- **Speaker points:** a score for each debater's speaking and arguing. See [Speaker points](speaker-points.md).
- **Speech document (speech doc):** the file of cards a debater will read. See [Speech docs](speech-docs.md).
- **Spreading:** speaking very fast to fit in more arguments.
- **SSD** (switch-side debate): the idea that debaters argue both sides over a tournament; a common standard in framework debates. See [Framework against a K aff](framework-vs-k-aff.md).
- **Standards:** reasons one interpretation is better, in topicality or theory. See [Standards](topicality-standards.md).
- **States counterplan:** a counterplan where the 50 states do the plan instead of the federal government. See [Example: states CP](example-states-cp.md).
- **Status quo (SQ):** the world now, without the plan.
- **Stealing prep:** preparing when your team is not using prep time and it is not the other team's speech or cross-ex. See [Prep time](prep-time.md).
- **Stock issues:** the basic jobs of the aff: harms, inherency, solvency, significance, topicality. See [Stock issues](stock-issues.md).
- **Structural violence:** harm built into how a society works (for example, poverty or exclusion), as opposed to a single event. See [Extinction vs structural violence](example-extinction-vs-structural.md).

## T

- **Tab room:** the tournament office that runs pairings and collects ballots. Ask them when the rules are unclear. See [Judging your first round](first-round-judge.md).
- **Tabula rasa:** "blank slate"; a judge who lets the debaters set the rules. See [Judge paradigms](judge-paradigms.md).
- **Tag:** the one-line claim a debater writes above a card.
- **Terminal impact:** the final harm at the end of a chain, such as loss of life, not a middle step such as a recession. See [Impacts](impacts.md).
- **Theory:** an argument about the rules of debate, such as whether a choice was fair. See [Theory](theory.md).
- **Thumper:** an event already in the status quo that should trigger the DA's link, so the DA is non-unique. See [Uniqueness](uniqueness.md).
- **Timeframe:** how soon an impact happens.
- **Topicality (T):** the argument that the plan does not fit the resolution. See [Topicality](topicality.md).
- **Turn:** an argument that flips the other team's point into a reason for your side.
- **Turns case:** an argument that one impact makes the other team's impact worse. See [Turns case](turns-case.md).
- **T-USFG:** a common name for framework against an aff that does not defend a plan by the government ("USFG" means the United States federal government). Some circuits use other names. See [Framework against a K aff](framework-vs-k-aff.md).
- **TVA** (topical version of the aff): a neg claim that the aff could read a plan within the topic and still make its main points. See [Framework against a K aff](framework-vs-k-aff.md).

## U

- **Uniqueness (U):** whether a DA's bad result is not happening now. See [Uniqueness](uniqueness.md).

## V

- **Violation:** how the plan breaks a topicality interpretation. See [Interpretation and violation](topicality-interpretation.md).
- **Voter:** a reason an argument should decide the round. See [Voters](topicality-voters.md).

## W

- **Warrant:** the reason a claim is true; the "because."
- **We meet:** the aff's answer that the plan meets the neg's interpretation.
