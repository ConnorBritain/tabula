# Links and link turns (L and L/T)

> **Plain version.** The link is the part of a disadvantage that ties the plan to the problem. It answers the question, "What does the plan do that starts the harm?" A good link is about this plan, not the topic in general. The aff can answer "no link" (the plan does not do that) or give a "link turn" (the plan does the opposite). A link turn can make the disadvantage a reason to vote for the plan.

## What a link is

A **disadvantage** (DA) says the plan causes something bad. It has four parts: uniqueness, link, internal link and impact. See [Disadvantages](disadvantages.md).

The **link** (L) is the first step of change. Uniqueness says the world is fine now. The link says the plan changes that. For example: "The plan costs $50 billion." Or: "The plan puts new rules on businesses."

The link is about cause. It must show that the *plan* does something, not that the topic area is risky in general.

## Kinds of links

Links differ in how close they are to the plan. They usually go from stronger to weaker in this order, but this is not a fixed rank:

| Kind | What it links to | Example (generic) |
|---|---|---|
| **Plan-specific** | This exact plan | "Experts say this program costs $50B." |
| **Mechanism** | How the plan works | "Any new rule on firms raises their costs." |
| **Agent** | Who acts | "When the federal government acts, states stop acting." |
| **Topic** | The whole topic area | "Any change in this area is risky." |

A topic link is the easiest to answer. The aff says, "That is about other plans, not ours." The neg makes a topic link stronger by tying it to the plan in cross-examination (CX), the questions after each constructive speech. "Does your plan require firms to do anything?" A "yes" turns a general link into a direct one.

## Link size

Links come in sizes. A plan that costs $1 million and a plan that costs $500 billion both "cost money." They do not push the world the same amount.

Link size works with uniqueness. If the world is far from the brink, or threshold (the point where the harm starts), the neg needs a big link. If the world is close to the brink, a small link can be enough. See [Uniqueness](uniqueness.md).

## Fiat and the link

**Fiat** is the idea that the aff gets to imagine the plan passes. So "the plan would never pass" is not a link. Most links come from what the plan does once it is in effect.

A few DAs link to the act of passing the plan. The best known is the politics DA: the fight to pass the plan costs a leader political support. Teams debate whether that kind of link is fair under fiat. See [Fiat](fiat.md) and [The politics DA](example-politics-da.md).

## How to answer a link

These answers are for the aff.

- **No link:** the plan does not do what the DA says. Point to your plan text or a card. This is defense: it makes the DA smaller.
- **Link is too small:** the plan does a little of it, but not enough to cross the brink.
- **Link turn (L/T):** the plan does the opposite. "The plan does not cost money; it saves money." If the aff wins a link turn, the plan *prevents* the harm. That is offense: a reason to vote aff.

A link turn is strongest with **non-unique**. If the harm is already coming, and the plan stops it, the DA becomes an advantage. If the harm is not coming, a link turn only prevents something that was not going to happen. This is why debaters say uniqueness controls the direction of the link.

These answers are for the neg, against a link turn.

- **The link turn is non-unique:** the good effect is already happening without the plan.
- **The link outweighs the turn:** the plan does both, but the harm is bigger or comes sooner.
- **The turn is not about this plan:** the aff's evidence is about a different kind of policy.

## The double turn

A **double turn** happens when the aff makes both a link turn and an impact turn on the same DA.

- A link turn says: "The plan prevents X."
- An **impact turn** says: "X is good."

Put them together, and the aff has argued that the plan prevents a good thing. That is a new reason to vote against the plan, made by the aff itself. The neg can concede both turns and extend them as offense. Many judges treat this as a strong neg argument if the neg points it out.

The fix is simple. On one DA, pick link turns or impact turns, not both. "No link" plus an impact turn is fine, because "no link" is defense. A link turn plus "no impact" is fine too, but it shrinks the turn's value.

## How to judge links

1. **Ask what the plan does.** Read the plan text and recall the CX. Does the plan do the thing the link says?
2. **Ask how much.** A small link may not cross the brink, if the aff argues that.
3. **Check uniqueness first.** It tells you whether the link or the link turn is the offense.
4. **Watch for double turns.** Many judges count one only if the neg points it out, and do not make the argument for them.

## Example in flow shorthand

A generic DA about business costs. The middle speeches are left out to save space. Each answer shows the speeches that argue it, top to bottom.

Key: L/T = link turn. -> = so. Names in parentheses are invented authors.

```
DA: BUSINESS COSTS

LINK
1NC: Plan adds rules on firms
     (Analyst 21)
2AC: 1. No link: plan sets
     goals, not rules
2NR: Plan text: 'require firms
     to' -> direct link (CX)
2AR: Even so, cost is tiny
     -> below threshold

LINK TURN
2AC: 2. L/T: clear rules cut
     legal costs (Expert 22)
2NR: L/T is non-unique: firms
     already get that saving
2AR: (no answer)
```

*How to read it:* the 2NR uses the plan text, quoted in CX, to show a direct link. The 2AR shifts to link size, but drops the point that the link turn is non-unique. So the link turn is no longer aff offense. The judge decides whether the small link is enough to cross the brink.

## Common mistakes

- **Neg: a link to the topic, not the plan.** Fix: use cross-ex to tie the link to the plan text.
- **Neg: ignoring link size.** Fix: explain why this plan pushes the world far enough to cause the harm.
- **Aff: a link turn with no uniqueness.** Fix: pair the turn with non-unique, so the plan stops a harm that is coming.
- **Aff: double-turning one DA.** Fix: pick link turns or impact turns on each DA, not both.
- **Aff: "it would never pass" as a no-link.** Fix: fiat already assumes the plan passes. Argue what the plan does.
- **Judges: counting a double turn the neg never named.** Fix: a judge who follows the flow closely weighs only what the teams argue.
