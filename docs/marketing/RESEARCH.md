# Marketing site research

This file collects design research for three Tabula sites.

- **tabulaspeech.com**: the parent brand. It shows plans, sign-in and all products.
- **tabuladebate.com**: the Tabula Debate product site and the free Debate Library.
- **tabulaforensics.com**: Tabula Speech Events. It is a coming-soon site for now.

Research date: November 2026.

## How we did the research

- We ran 12 searches in Mobbin. Mobbin is a library of screenshots from real apps and sites.
- We read 16 live sites with a web fetch tool.
- We did not invent numbers or quotes. A short quote is in quotation marks and has a source.
- "Mobbin: X" means a screenshot of site X in Mobbin.

## 1. Hero: explain the product in one screen

### What the best sites do

- They state what the product is in one short line.
- They add one subline that says who it is for and the key benefit.
- They show one primary button and one secondary link.
- They show the real product under or beside the text.

### Examples

- **Linear** uses a one-line headline and a one-line subline. It has two buttons: "Get started" and "Contact sales" (https://linear.app).
- **Plausible** says what it replaces, then "No cookies, just insights." The buttons are "Start free trial" and "View live demo" (https://plausible.io).
- **Mobbin: Sketch** ("Sketch For Education") puts "free as a student or educator" in the first line of body text.
- **Mobbin: Miro** uses the headline "Free digital whiteboard for online education". The word "Free" comes first.
- **Mobbin: Granola** puts three short benefit rows under the headline. Each row has one icon and one line. One row says "Private by default".

### What to copy for Tabula

- Headline: say the job in plain words. Example shape: "Judge and flow policy debate rounds."
- Subline: name the audience and the two key facts. Example shape: "For parent judges, coaches and debaters. Free. Your rounds stay on your device."
- Primary button: "Open Tabula Debate". Secondary link: "Read the Debate Library".
- Put three fact rows under the buttons, like Granola: "Free with your own AI key", "Saved on your device", "No ads, no tracking".
- On tabulaspeech.com, the hero sells the family. It names Debate and Speech Events and links to each.

### What to avoid

- Do not use a clever slogan that does not say what the app does.
- Do not use stock photos of students. Parent judges must see the app, not a mood.
- Do not show more than two buttons.
- Do not name an AI company in the hero.

## 2. Product shot style

### What the best sites do

- They show real UI, not an abstract drawing.
- They crop to the part that matters.
- Some offer a live demo next to the static image.

### Examples

- **Linear** shows a realistic issue view next to a board. It adds small labelled callouts (https://linear.app).
- **Plausible** shows a static dashboard and links a live demo (https://plausible.io).
- **Mobbin: Evernote** puts a list of features on the left. The screenshot on the right changes with the feature.
- **Mobbin: Dub** shows the real app box in the hero. The URL "app.dub.co" is visible.
- **Things** shows the app on several devices. It favours device range over UI detail (https://culturedcode.com/things/).

### What to copy for Tabula

- Use a real flow sheet with real-looking arguments. Use the dark Flow look and the light look.
- Use the Evernote pattern for features: a short list on the left, one screenshot on the right.
- Show the app on a laptop and a phone. Judges use both at tournaments.
- Offer "Try a sample round" that opens the app with demo data. This is the Plausible live-demo idea.
- Export screenshots as WebP or AVIF with a set width and height. This stops layout shift.

### What to avoid

- Do not use heavy device frames. They add weight and hide the UI.
- Do not use fake data that looks silly to debaters. Use correct debate terms.
- Do not autoplay video in the hero.

## 3. Feature sections

### What the best sites do

- Each section has one heading, one or two lines, and one image.
- Sections move fast. They alternate claim, picture and proof.

### Examples

- **Things**: each section has an icon, a short heading, a short blurb and a link (https://culturedcode.com/things/).
- **Mobbin: Monologue** uses tabs ("Dictation", "Voice notes", "Meetings") above one large image.
- **Mobbin: Canva** ("Canva for Education at a glance") uses four icon tiles in one row.
- **Kahoot schools** groups use cases by grade, subject and activity (https://kahoot.com/schools/).

### What to copy for Tabula

- Group features by role, like NSDA does: "For judges", "For coaches", "For debaters".
- Use tabs like Monologue for the three roles. One image per tab.
- Keep each feature to one heading and one sentence.
- Add a short "How the AI works" block. Say: bring your own key for free, or use a paid plan with no key. Say program admins can turn AI off for students.

### What to avoid

- Do not list every feature on the home page. Link to a full feature page.
- Do not use jargon like "AI-powered workflow". Say what the user gets.

## 4. Pricing page

### What the best sites do

- The free tier is real and clear. It is not a trial in disguise.
- Paid tiers say exactly what you get for the money.
- School plans have their own column or page.
- A FAQ answers money questions in plain words.

### Examples

- **Obsidian** calls the core app "Free without limits". No sign-up is needed. Paid items are add-ons (https://obsidian.md/pricing).
- **Obsidian** says commercial use does not need payment. It asks organisations to buy a license to keep the project independent.
- **Raycast**: the free plan excludes AI. AI comes in paid plans. Users can turn AI off in Settings (https://www.raycast.com/pricing).
- **Mobbin: Miro** and **Mobbin: Mural** name tiers by audience: Student, Educator or Classroom, Institution. The last tier says "Contact us".
- **Mobbin: Canva** FAQ asks "Is Canva for Education really free?" and answers it at once.
- **Mobbin: Glide** pricing FAQ includes "Are there special discounts for nonprofits and schools?"
- **Mobbin: Dovetail** pricing FAQ includes "Can we cancel any time?" and "Can we pay via invoice?"

### What to copy for Tabula

- Four columns: Free, Pass, Program, and Pro (later). Mark Pro as "Coming later". Do not hide it.
- Free column: "Everything in the app. Bring your own AI key." Say it never expires.
- Pass column: "Hosted AI. No key needed."
- Program column: for coaches. List admin controls, such as "Turn AI off for students".
- Show one plain sentence above the columns: "The app is free and open source. Paid plans pay for hosted AI."
- Add a FAQ with these questions:
  - Is the free plan really free?
  - What is an AI key and where do I get one?
  - Where are my rounds saved?
  - Can I cancel any time?
  - Can my school pay by invoice or purchase order?
  - Can I turn AI off for my students?
  - Is the code open source? Where is it?
- Link to the source code and the AGPL license from the FAQ.

### What to avoid

- Do not use fake "was" prices or strike-through prices. GitHub on Mobbin shows this pattern; do not copy it.
- Do not pre-select yearly billing to make the price look low.
- Do not use countdown timers or "limited time" banners.
- Do not ask for a card to use the free tier.
- Do not hide the cancel path.
- Do not compare AI companies or recommend one.

## 5. Social proof for schools and coaches

### What the best sites do

- They show real quotes with a name, a role and a school.
- They match the quote to the reader. Teachers see teacher quotes.

### Examples

- **Mobbin: Duolingo** ("hear from our teachers"): three cards. Each card has a photo, a quote, a name and "High School ... teacher".
- **Mobbin: Grammarly**: each quote has the school logo, the person, and the job title.
- **Mobbin: Craft**: one App Store review quote, credited to the reviewer name.
- **Kahoot schools** uses teacher quotes plus privacy seals (https://kahoot.com/schools/).

### What to do with no logos yet

- Show proof of work, not proof of fame:
  - "79 free Debate Library pages." This is a true count.
  - A link to the public source code and its change log.
  - A short "Built by a former debater for the debate community" note, if true. Do not name the owner.
- Show the Debate Library itself as proof. Free, useful content earns trust.
- Ask early coaches for one-line quotes. Get written consent. Use first name, role and state only if they ask for less.
- Add a "Used at" row only after real programs agree to be listed.

### What to avoid

- Do not invent quotes, user counts or school logos.
- Do not show logos of NSDA or Tabroom. That suggests a partnership that does not exist.
- Do not use star ratings without a real source.

## 6. Trust and student-data privacy page

### What the best sites do

- They write privacy in plain words, not only in legal text.
- They list what they collect and what they never collect.
- They point to the code so others can check.

### Examples

- **Plausible data policy** has a table of data points and a list of what it does not collect. It says "The code is public and auditable" (https://plausible.io/data-policy).
- **Obsidian** says notes are stored locally and the apps collect no telemetry (https://obsidian.md/pricing).
- **Figma for Education** FAQ says only a name and email are required. It says where data is hosted and how deletion works (https://www.figma.com/education/).
- **Mobbin: Canva** has a "Trust" hub with tabs: Overview, Security, Privacy, Legal.
- **Mobbin: Maze** uses six small cards, one per security fact.
- **Mobbin: Clay** opens its policy with "Privacy and security are too important for legalese."

### School trust signals

- **Common Sense Privacy**: a Pass rating means no third-party tracking or marketing, no profiling and no targeted ads (https://www.commonsensemedia.org/kids-action/articles/protecting-kids-digital-privacy-is-now-easier-than-ever). A product fails if no privacy policy is readable before sign-up (https://privacy.commonsense.org/resource/tier-risks).
- **Student Privacy Pledge**: FPF retired it on April 25, 2025. Do not claim to sign it. FPF points to the K-12 Secure by Design Pledge and the Student Data Privacy Consortium agreement instead (https://fpf.org/student-privacy-pledge/).
- **Kahoot** shows a Common Sense privacy seal and other seals on its schools page (https://kahoot.com/schools/).

### What to copy for Tabula

- Make one page: "Privacy and trust". Link it from every footer and from pricing.
- Open with three facts:
  - "Your rounds are saved on your device."
  - "No ads. No tracking scripts."
  - "The code is open source. Anyone can check it."
- Add two lists: "What we keep" and "What we never keep". Use the Plausible table style.
- Add a section "For coaches and schools". Say admins can turn AI off for students. Say what the AI provider receives when AI is on.
- Explain AI data flow in one diagram: device, then AI provider, then device. Do not name a provider as best.
- Put the privacy policy where people can read it before sign-up. This matches the Common Sense Fail rule.
- Later, ask Common Sense for an evaluation. Show the seal only after a real rating.

### What to avoid

- Do not say "100% secure" or "military-grade".
- Do not show seals you do not have.
- Do not hide AI data use in the legal text only.

## 7. Sign-in entry points

### What the best sites do

- "Sign in" or "Log in" is a text link at the top right.
- The sign-up button sits next to it and has more weight.
- The sign-in page is calm: logo, one heading, email field, one button.

### Examples

- **Proton**: "Sign in" is a text link at top right, next to a "Create a free account" button (https://proton.me).
- **Tabroom**: "Login" sits at top right, with a login block near the top (https://www.tabroom.com). Debate parents know this pattern.
- **Mobbin: Notion**: email field first, "Continue", then other options in small tiles.
- **Mobbin: Plane**: three provider buttons, then email, then terms in small text.
- **Mobbin: Dub**: the app lives on a separate host, "app.dub.co".

### What to copy for Tabula

- Put "Sign in" at top right on all three sites. Use the same words everywhere.
- Point all "Sign in" links to one account page on tabulaspeech.com, or one app host. Pick one and keep it.
- Use a separate app host, for example an "app." subdomain. Keep marketing pages and the app apart. This keeps the marketing pages fast and script-free.
- Say on the sign-in page: "You do not need an account to use the free app." This is true for local-first use.
- Keep the sign-in page to email first. Add passkey later.

### What to avoid

- Do not force sign-in to try the app.
- Do not use different words ("Log in", "Sign in", "Account") on different sites.
- Do not add third-party sign-in buttons that load tracking scripts on the marketing pages.

## 8. Parent brand with product lines

### What the best sites do

- The hub names each product with one line and a link.
- Each product has its own site and its own voice.
- Nav and footer share a frame, so users know they are in one family.

### Examples

- **37signals**: the hub lists Basecamp, HEY, Fizzy and ONCE as plain links. Each link goes to its own domain (https://37signals.com).
- **Proton**: one "Products" mega-menu lists each app with an icon and a short line. Each block has "Get it for free" and "Explore" (https://proton.me).
- **Mobbin: Superhuman** footer has a "Products" column: Superhuman Go, Grammarly, Coda, Superhuman Mail.
- **Mobbin: 1Password** footer puts "1Password products" as the first column.

### What to copy for Tabula

- tabulaspeech.com home: one card per product. Card: icon, name, one line, status ("Available" or "Coming soon"), one link.
- Add a small "Tabula products" menu in the header of all three sites. Use the Proton mega-menu idea, but keep it to three items.
- Use the same header height, logo position, font and footer on all three sites. Change only the accent and product name.
- The parent site uses black, white and gray with a purple accent and small gold accents. Product sites can keep their own accent.
- Plans and sign-in live on tabulaspeech.com. Product sites link there.

### What to avoid

- Do not make product sites copy the hub's corporate tone. tabuladebate.com speaks to parents and debaters.
- Do not hide which company makes a product. Put "A Tabula product" in each product footer.

## 9. Coming-soon page (tabulaforensics.com)

### What the best sites do

- They show one sentence, one email field and one button.
- They say what will happen to the email.

### Examples

- **Mobbin: Base**: "Get on the waitlist", one email field, "Get early access", and a privacy policy link under the button.
- **Mobbin: Pally**: one line, "Be the first to know when Pally becomes available."
- **Mobbin: ISO Meet**: one email field and one button. It also has a social proof count.
- **Mobbin: Sana**: asks for name, company and job title. This is too much for us.

### What to copy for Tabula

- Headline: "Tabula Speech Events is coming."
- One line: name the events. "For extemp, oratory, interp and Congress."
- One email field and one button: "Tell me when it opens."
- Under the button: "One email when it opens. No other mail." Link to the privacy page.
- Add a link back: "Use Tabula Debate today."
- Add an optional field: "Which event do you coach or judge?" Keep it optional.

### What to avoid

- Do not ask for school, role and phone on day one.
- Do not show a launch date you cannot meet.
- Do not add a fake signup counter.

## 10. Footer

### What the best sites do

- The footer has columns: Products, Resources, Company, Legal.
- A bottom row holds the copyright, legal links and theme or language controls.

### Examples

- **Mobbin: Calendly**: a short statement, four columns, a downloads row, then a legal row with "Cookie Settings" and "Your Privacy Choices".
- **Mobbin: Loom**: six columns, then social icons and "Privacy for Humans" in the legal row.
- **Proton**: tagline "Privacy by default", four columns, a utility row (language, status), then a legal row (https://proton.me).
- **NSDA**: contact, FAQ, terms, privacy, status page and social icons (https://www.speechanddebate.org).

### What to copy for Tabula

- Use one shared footer on all three sites with four columns:
  - **Products**: Tabula Debate, Tabula Speech Events (coming soon), Plans.
  - **Learn**: Debate Library, Start here, Help.
  - **Trust**: Privacy and trust, Open source code, License (AGPL), Accessibility.
  - **Company**: About, Contact, Change log.
- Bottom row: copyright, theme switch (light, dark, system), and a one-line note: "No ads. No tracking scripts."
- Keep the footer short. Five links per column at most.

### What to avoid

- Do not add a cookie banner if you set no tracking cookies. A banner suggests tracking.
- Do not copy dense legal footers like Mobbin: Slash.

## 11. Accessibility and speed

### What the best sites do

- They state an accessibility target in public.
- They keep pages light. Text loads first.

### Examples

- **Kahoot schools** claims WCAG 2.2 AA (https://kahoot.com/schools/). WCAG is the web standard for accessibility.
- **Plausible** says its script stores nothing on the device (https://plausible.io/data-policy).
- **Tabroom** is text-first and dense (https://www.tabroom.com). Coaches are used to fast, plain pages.

### What to copy for Tabula

- Target WCAG 2.2 AA on all three sites. Publish an accessibility page with known gaps.
- Use static HTML and CSS. Add JavaScript only where it helps, such as tabs and the theme switch.
- Use system fonts or one self-hosted font. Do not load fonts from a third-party host.
- Keep each page small. Set width and height on every image.
- Give every image alt text. Give every screenshot a short caption.
- Check colour contrast for the purple and gold accents on both light and dark.
- Make all controls work with the keyboard. Show a clear focus ring.
- Respect "reduced motion" settings.

### What to avoid

- Do not use gold for body text or small text. Gold on white often fails contrast.
- Do not use scroll-jacking or heavy animation.
- Do not load chat widgets or analytics scripts.

## 12. Dark mode

### What the best sites do

- They follow the system setting by default.
- Product shots change with the theme.

### Examples

- **Mobbin: Monologue** and **Mobbin: Profound** use dark-first marketing pages with app screenshots that match.
- **Mobbin: Clay** uses a dark help page with high-contrast text.
- **Linear** shows a dark product UI on its home page (https://linear.app).

### What to copy for Tabula

- Follow the system theme. Add a manual switch in the footer.
- Use two sets of screenshots: light app on the light site, Flow look on the dark site. Use the HTML picture element to swap them.
- Use colour tokens: one set for light, one for dark. Purple and gold must pass contrast in both.
- tabulaspeech.com can lean dark (black and gray). tabuladebate.com can default light for parent judges, with dark on request.

### What to avoid

- Do not invert images. Make real dark screenshots.
- Do not use pure black with pure white body text. Use a dark gray and an off-white to reduce glare.

## What the debate audience is used to

- **Tabroom** is dense and text-first. "Login" is at the top right. It lists tournaments, deadlines and judge paradigms (https://www.tabroom.com).
- **NSDA** uses role paths: students, coaches, judges, district leaders. Parents come in as judges (https://www.speechanddebate.org).
- Parent judges arrive in a hurry, often on a phone, often the night before a tournament.
- Lesson: make "I am a parent judge" a first-class path. Link it from the hero on tabuladebate.com. Send it to "Start here" in the Debate Library.

## Top 12 rules for Tabula sites

1. Say what the app does in the first line. No slogans.
2. Show the real app. Use real debate terms in screenshots.
3. Put "Free", "Saved on your device" and "No ads, no tracking" above the fold.
4. Make the free tier real. No card. No expiry. No trial language.
5. Explain the AI choice in one line: your own key for free, or a paid plan with no key.
6. Never name, rank or favour one AI company.
7. Never invent quotes, counts, logos or seals. Show the Debate Library and the open code as proof.
8. Keep one plain "Privacy and trust" page. Link it from every footer and from pricing.
9. Put "Sign in" at the top right on all three sites, with the same words and one destination.
10. Share one header frame and one footer across all three sites. Change only the accent and product name.
11. Keep pages static, light and script-free. Target WCAG 2.2 AA in light and dark.
12. Keep the coming-soon page to one line, one email field and one promise about that email.
